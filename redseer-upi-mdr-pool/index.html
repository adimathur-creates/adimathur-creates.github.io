The 0.4% Question

From 15 October 2026, larger merchants pay a 0.4% fee on UPI payments above ₹2,000, the first UPI merchant fee since 2020. How big is the revenue pool, how much do the exemptions take out, and what does it mean for payment companies, large merchants and their investors?

Aditya Mathur · October 2026 · written the way I would brief an engagement manager in week one

Read this first. I am not affiliated with Redseer, NPCI or any company named. The rule and the market totals are public and sourced below. The split of high-value UPI payments across exempt and special-rate merchants is not published; those shares are my assumptions, marked as such, and the research plan in section 7 is how I would replace them. The model runs live on this page and in the Excel model.
Executive summary
  1. The pool is about ₹14,000 crore a year at today's run-rate, half the ₹29,000 crore headline. Exemptions and special rates take out the other half.
  2. The answer turns on who is exempt, not on the rate. Four segment-mix assumptions move the estimate more than growth or the ₹300 cap. NPCI has not published them, so they are what primary research must pin down.
  3. It covers most of UPI's merchant-payment cost for the first time. In the base case it is about 7 times this year's budget incentive and covers about two-thirds of the ₹20,700 crore the industry says P2M costs it.

Exhibit 6 · Implications

Three clients, three different questions

Payment aggregator

A new revenue line, if they can keep it

Online merchants mostly take far more than ₹1 lakh a month, so much of their above-₹2,000 UPI volume is likely chargeable. The open questions are how much of the 0.4% reaches the aggregator after the bank's share, and whether competition prices it below the rate.

Large merchant CFO

₹40 lakh per ₹100 crore of high-ticket UPI sales

A new cost where there was none. UPI likely stays cheaper than cards, so the more useful work is finding which categories carry the most above-₹2,000 value and whether any of it qualifies for a special rate.

Investor

The pool is real; who keeps it is not settled

Value will flow to whoever holds the merchant relationship on high-ticket online categories: travel, education, electronics, B2B. Diligence should wait on the split, and test it in expert calls before it is published.

These are hypotheses for the research to confirm, not findings. The fee split between participants is not public; one secondary report mentions a proposed 40% bank share, which I have not been able to verify.

15 expert interviews
  • 4 acquiring or merchant-banking heads at banks
  • 4 pricing or product leads at payment aggregators
  • 3 payment heads at large merchants (travel, education, electronics)
  • 2 at AMCs or brokers
  • 2 former NPCI or regulator-facing staff
Merchant survey, n = 400
  • Quotas by category, city tier and monthly UPI collections
  • Share of sales above ₹2,000 on UPI
  • Planned response: absorb, reprice, steer to other modes
  • Payment provider and how they were told of the fee
Interview guide (excerpt)
  1. What share of your merchants' UPI value is above ₹2,000?
  2. Of that, how much sits with merchants under ₹1 lakh a month?
  3. How are you pricing the 0.4% to merchants?
  4. What share of the fee do you expect to keep?
  5. Which categories are asking for special rates?

Limits
  • The 66% high-value share comes from June 2025 data; the mix may have shifted since.
  • The segment shares are assumptions. The ranges in Exhibit 5 show how much they matter.
  • Revenue is a run-rate on FY27 value. It ignores ramp-up in the first months and any change in behaviour beyond the leakage assumption.
  • The fee split between participants is unknown, so I do not size any one company's share.
