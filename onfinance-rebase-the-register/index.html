Rebase the Register

RBI repealed about 5,600 circulars and folded about 3,800 more into 244 Master Directions. Every new ComplianceOS client still arrives with a compliance tracker that cites the old ones. This is the forward-deployed step that turns that tracker into a clean obligation register on day zero, without silently dropping a single live obligation.

Aditya Mathur · October 2026 · prepared for the OnFinance AI team

Read this first. I do not work at OnFinance AI and have no client data. RBI's consolidation numbers are real and sourced below. The clause library and the client tracker are synthetic: 455 obligations across 12 themes, 1,876 legacy circulars and a 1,321-row tracker. The noise rates (missing references, typos, stale citations, duplicates) are my assumptions, listed on this page. The matching runs live in your browser.
1 · THE CLIENT PROBLEM

The rulebook changed under every tracker in India

ComplianceOS already knows which legacy circular went into which Master Direction clause. A client's own tracker does not. It is years of rows typed by different teams: references in five formats, some missing, some mistyped, some citing an older circular on the same subject that RBI has now repealed. Before the client can go live, every row must end up in one of four places:

OutcomeMeaningCost of getting it wrong
Re-pointThe obligation lives on in a Master Direction clauseWrong clause: wrong owner, wrong evidence
CarryThe circular continues as a standalone instructionSame as above
RetireRepealed as obsolete; record whyIf the obligation is actually live, it disappears from the register. Nobody notices until an inspection.
MergeTwo rows now point at the same clauseDuplicate work and conflicting answers

How does an FDE re-baseline a new client's tracker in days instead of weeks, with zero live obligations silently retired, and show the client what their old tracker never covered?

2 · THREE WAYS TO DO IT

Fast and wrong, slow and wrong, or neither

ApproachAnalyst hoursRows rightLive obligations lost

Hand-research error assumed at 3% of rows. Ladder accuracy assumes analysts get review rows right; the day-7 spot-check is how that is proven at a real client. Across five stress runs the ladder's automatic decisions were 99.8–100% right.

Why "let the AI map it" fails. A text matcher alone has no way to know a row is live when its wording is thin, and no way to know a row is dead when it reads like a live subject. It retires live obligations and keeps dead ones. The same model is safe when rules go first and it only handles what the rules cannot.

3 · RULES FIRST, TEXT SECOND, PEOPLE LAST

The decision ladder

StepDone byRule
1. Normalise the referenceRules"rbi 2016-17 / 45", "Circ. 45 (2016-17)" and "RBI-2016-17-45" all become one key
2. Look it up in the consolidation mapRulesSubsumed, standalone or repealed, straight from the mapping ComplianceOS already holds
3. Check the text agreesText matchRe-point only if the row's wording matches the target clause. A reference that disagrees with its own text goes to a person
4. Two keys to retireRules + TextRetire only if the reference is repealed and the text matches that repealed circular clearly better than any live clause. This catches stale citations
5. Typo repairRules + TextA one-digit-off reference is repaired only if exactly one neighbour's text clearly matches
6. No referenceText matchMay re-point above a confidence threshold with a clear margin. Text alone never retires
7. Everything elseAnalystReviewed with the suggested clause and the reason shown. About 3 minutes a row instead of 9
4 · RUN THE RE-BASELINE

Change the levers, watch what breaks

Stress test. I reran the ladder on three different client trackers, one 50% messier and one 40% cleaner. It lost 0–1 live obligations in every run, against 33–77 for the text matcher alone and 3–7 for a careful analyst working by hand. Review hours ranged from 7 to 19, against about 200 by hand. Without the two-key rule, 9–35 live obligations were retired, almost all of them stale citations.

7 · HOW I WOULD RUN IT AT A CLIENT

Ten working days, inside onboarding

Day 1Kick-off with the Chief Compliance Officer. Get the tracker export, the policy library, the list of owners and the last inspection's findings.
Day 2Profile the tracker: row count, reference formats, missing references, owners who have left. Agree the four outcomes and the review rules in writing.
Day 3Run the ladder. Send the analyst review queue, each row with its suggested clause and reason.
Days 4–6Compliance reviews the queue; I sit with them. Every override is logged and reused to tune the thresholds for this client.
Day 7Spot-check: the CCO's team picks 50 auto-decided rows at random and checks them. Acceptance: zero live obligations retired.
Day 8Gap report by department, with owners, signed off by the CCO. Owners reassigned for rows whose owner has left.
Days 9–10Load the clean register into ComplianceOS. Every retired row keeps its reason in the audit log. Hand over to steady state.
Questions for the CCO on day 1
  1. Which tracker is the real one, and who else keeps their own?
  2. How do teams cite a circular today: number, date or subject?
  3. Which obligations came up in the last inspection?
  4. Who can sign off retiring an obligation?
  5. Which departments have changed owners in the last year?
Acceptance criteria
  • Every row has an outcome and a written reason
  • Zero live obligations retired on the 50-row spot-check
  • Gap report signed by the CCO
  • Every obligation has a current owner
8 · THE 5-MINUTE DEMO TO A CCO

Their tracker, not ours

  1. Upload their tracker live. "1,321 rows. Here is how many cite circulars RBI has repealed."
  2. Show one stale citation. The reference says repealed, the obligation is live, and the ladder stopped it. "This is the row that becomes an inspection finding."
  3. Show the review queue. A few hundred rows, each with a suggestion. "Your team reviews these, not all 1,321."
  4. Open the gap report. "These obligations apply to you and were never on your tracker."
  5. Open the audit log for one retired row: the reference, the rule and the reason.
9 · WHAT I WOULD HAND BACK TO PRODUCT

Do it once by hand, then make it a feature

  • A register importer in ComplianceOS: upload a tracker, get the four outcomes, the review queue and the gap report. Every client onboarding starts here.
  • "Live obligations lost" as a release gate for any change to matching, tested on a golden set of past client trackers.
  • Per-client thresholds learned from overrides during review, so the second migration at a client is faster than the first.
  • The same ladder for policies. A bank's policy documents cite repealed circulars too. Same lookup, same two-key rule.
10 · LIMITS

What this does not prove

  • The library and tracker are synthetic. Real clause wording is longer and messier, which favours a stronger text model than the TF-IDF stand-in used here.
  • Noise rates are assumptions: 13% missing references, 5% typos, 6% stale citations, 7% duplicates. The day-2 profiling replaces them with the client's real rates.
  • I assume analysts decide review rows correctly. In practice the spot-check is what proves it.
  • Applicability is simplified: every clause applies to the client. Real entity-type and layer filters come from ComplianceOS.
