No expired item leaves a dark store: an expiry-safe inventory system for Zepto

In August 2026, Maharashtra's food regulator inspected 86 quick-commerce sites and suspended 14 licences, five of them Zepto's; items past their use-by date were found at a Zepto store in Mulund. With 1,139 dark stores, nearly 50,000 SKUs per city and 2.3 million orders a day, no store manager can read every label. This is how I'd make expiry a field the warehouse system knows, uses and enforces, from the mother hub to the picker's hand.

The problem

Expired stock reaches shelves and customers because the system often doesn't know the expiry date of what's on the shelf, so nothing downstream can act on it.

The insight

Blocking expired items at pick only works for batches whose date was captured at inbound. Capture is the foundation; every other fix depends on it.

The plan

Capture dates at the mother hub and carry them to the store, slot one batch per bin, guide FEFO picks, block expired picks, generate the daily sweep, and mark down near-expiry stock instead of binning it.

2. The problem statement

Across 1,139 dark stores, expired and near-expiry stock is found by people reading labels, when they have time. That's why it reaches shelves, inspectors and customers. Make expiry a field the warehouse system captures at inbound, carries to the store and enforces at pick, so that expired items delivered per million orders goes to zero, near-expiry stock is sold rather than binned, and picking stays as fast as it is today.

3. Where an expired item comes from

1

Inbound without a date

At the mother hub, a case is received against a purchase order by SKU and quantity. Under time pressure, the batch's expiry date often isn't recorded. Everything downstream inherits that blind spot.

2

Batches mixed in one bin

A new delivery goes into a bin that still holds an older batch. The system sees one SKU with one quantity; the shelf holds two dates.

3

Front-of-shelf picking

A picker racing a 76-second packing target takes whatever is nearest, usually the newest stock. The oldest batch moves to the back and quietly expires.

4

Sweeps that slip

Expiry checks are a manual walk. On a busy day they get skipped, and with thousands of date-sensitive SKUs nobody catches everything anyway.

5

Returns put back on the shelf

A returned food item goes back into stock without a date or integrity check. (Swiggy Instamart received FSSAI notices in July 2026 over infant formula allegedly resupplied after a return.)

4. The fix, stage by stage through the warehouse system

StageWhat changesWhy it works
Inbound (mother hub)Capture expiry per batch at receipt: scan the GS1 barcode's expiry field (AI 17) where suppliers print it; read the printed date with the camera where they don't; manual entry only as a last resort for food categories. The date travels to the dark store in the shipment notice, so the store never re-enters it.One capture at the hub covers dozens of stores. It has to add under 5 seconds per case or it won't happen.
Putaway (dark store)One batch per bin for date-sensitive SKUs; a newer batch goes to an adjacent bin, and the app says which.The system's record matches the shelf, so the picker's instruction can be precise.
PickThe pick list directs to the oldest batch (FEFO). If the scanned batch is past its date, the app blocks it: "Set aside, pick from bin B-14 instead", in the picker's language, one tap.The last line of defence, and the only one that protects the customer even when a sweep was missed.
Daily sweepThe system generates a short list each morning: batches within their pull window, sorted by aisle, with the action for each (mark down, return to hub, discard with photo).Turns a full-store walk into a 10-minute task that actually gets done.
Near-expiryItems inside a markdown window are offered at a discount in the app with the date shown; if unsold, routed back to the hub for donation or liquidation.Recovers value from stock that would otherwise be binned, which pays for catching more of it.
ReturnsReturned food is quarantined until scanned for date and seal; it can't re-enter sellable stock otherwise.Closes the path regulators have already cited at a competitor.
Store scoreA daily expiry-readiness score per store for city managers: share of date-sensitive stock with a known date, sweep completed, blocks, items found expired.Gives managers something to manage, and compliance a view before an inspector arrives.

6. Metrics

  • North star: expired items delivered per million orders. Measured from customer complaints with photos plus a weekly random audit of packed orders. Target: zero.
  • What drives it: share of date-sensitive on-hand stock with a known expiry; share of picks that take the oldest batch; sweep completion rate; pick-time blocks per 1,000 picks (falls as upstream fixes work).
  • Money: write-off per store per day; value recovered through markdowns; return-to-hub volume.
  • Guardrails: picker seconds per item (the block must not slow picking); inbound seconds per case at the hub; item fill rate and stockouts (pulling stock too early empties shelves); inspection findings per store.

7. Rollout and test design

  • Randomise by store, not by order. Shelves and staff are shared inside a store, so orders can't be split into test and control. Pick 40 Mumbai stores (where the regulator acted), pair them by orders per day and category mix, and switch on the changes in one store of each pair. Read results as difference-in-differences against the control stores.
  • Shadow first. For two weeks the pick-time block runs silently: it logs what it would have blocked without interrupting pickers. That sizes the problem with real data and tunes the rules before any picker sees a message.
  • Inbound before store. Date capture starts at the two hubs feeding the pilot stores, because nothing downstream works without it.
  • Exit criteria to scale: zero expired deliveries found in audits for 4 weeks; picker seconds per item unchanged within 5%; fill rate unchanged; net write-off (after markdowns) at or below control.

8. Getting it used on the ground

Who has to change, and what makes them
  • Hub inbound teams: scan, don't type. If capture slows receipt, it gets skipped.
  • Category and procurement: ask top suppliers to print GS1 date barcodes over two quarters; track coverage by supplier.
  • Pickers: one-tap "set aside", local language, never a dead end.
  • Store managers: the daily score, and a 10-minute sweep list instead of a full-store walk.
  • Finance: told in advance that write-offs rise before markdowns catch up.
Milestones
  • Weeks 1–2: measure today: date coverage, FEFO rate, sweep completion in 10 stores
  • Weeks 3–6: hub date capture and shipment-notice dates live for pilot hubs
  • Weeks 5–8: one batch per bin, FEFO guidance, block in shadow mode
  • Weeks 9–12: block enforced, sweep list and markdowns live; read the test
  • Quarter 2: city by city, starting where inspections are most active

9. SQL

PostgreSQL. Assumed tables: inventory_batches (store_id, sku_id, batch_id, bin_id, qty, expiry_date, received_at), skus (category, is_date_sensitive), picks, pick_blocks, sweep_tasks, write_offs, markdown_sales.

1. How much date-sensitive stock has no known expiry, by store and category 
SELECT b.store_id, s.category,
       SUM(b.qty)                                                          AS units_on_hand,
       ROUND(100.0 * SUM(CASE WHEN b.expiry_date IS NULL THEN b.qty ELSE 0 END) / NULLIF(SUM(b.qty), 0), 1) AS pct_no_expiry
FROM inventory_batches b
JOIN skus s USING (sku_id)
WHERE s.is_date_sensitive AND b.qty > 0
GROUP BY 1, 2
ORDER BY pct_no_expiry DESC;
2. Tomorrow's sweep list: what to pull, in aisle order 
SELECT b.store_id, b.bin_id, s.sku_name, b.batch_id, b.qty, b.expiry_date,
       CASE WHEN b.expiry_date < CURRENT_DATE + 1 THEN 'DISCARD (photo)'
            WHEN b.expiry_date <= CURRENT_DATE + s.markdown_window_days THEN 'MARK DOWN'
            ELSE 'RETURN TO HUB' END                                        AS action
FROM inventory_batches b
JOIN skus s USING (sku_id)
WHERE b.qty > 0
  AND b.expiry_date <= CURRENT_DATE + s.pull_window_days
ORDER BY b.store_id, b.bin_id;
3. FEFO violations: picks of a newer batch while an older one was in stock 
SELECT p.store_id, DATE(p.picked_at) AS day,
       COUNT(*)                                                           AS picks,
       ROUND(100.0 * AVG(CASE WHEN EXISTS (
           SELECT 1 FROM inventory_batches o
           WHERE o.store_id = p.store_id AND o.sku_id = p.sku_id AND o.qty > 0
             AND o.expiry_date < pb.expiry_date AND o.received_at <= p.picked_at)
         THEN 1 ELSE 0 END), 1)                                           AS pct_not_oldest
FROM picks p
JOIN inventory_batches pb ON pb.batch_id = p.batch_id
WHERE p.picked_at >= CURRENT_DATE - 7
GROUP BY 1, 2 ORDER BY pct_not_oldest DESC;
4. Blocks at pick: how often, and is the problem shrinking? 
SELECT DATE_TRUNC('week', k.blocked_at)                                   AS week,
       k.store_id,
       COUNT(*)                                                           AS blocks,
       ROUND(1000.0 * COUNT(*) / NULLIF((SELECT COUNT(*) FROM picks p
             WHERE p.store_id = k.store_id
               AND DATE_TRUNC('week', p.picked_at) = DATE_TRUNC('week', k.blocked_at)), 0), 2) AS blocks_per_1000_picks
FROM pick_blocks k
GROUP BY 1, 2 ORDER BY 1 DESC, 4 DESC;
5. Write-off vs recovery, by store and reason 
SELECT w.store_id, w.reason,                     -- 'EXPIRED', 'NEAR_EXPIRY_UNSOLD', 'DAMAGED', 'RETURN_REJECTED'
       SUM(w.value_inr)                                                   AS written_off_inr,
       COALESCE(SUM(m.recovered_inr), 0)                                  AS recovered_inr
FROM write_offs w
LEFT JOIN markdown_sales m ON m.store_id = w.store_id AND m.sale_date = w.write_off_date
WHERE w.write_off_date >= CURRENT_DATE - 30
GROUP BY 1, 2 ORDER BY written_off_inr DESC;

11. Risks and limits

  • Suppliers without date barcodes. Camera reading of printed dates will make mistakes. Low-confidence reads go to a person; supplier coverage is tracked and pushed through category teams.
  • Picker friction. If the block slows picking, people find ways around it. Shadow mode first, and picker seconds per item as a hard guardrail.
  • Pulling too early empties shelves. Pull windows are set per category and checked against fill rate.
  • Markdowns can train customers to wait. Keep them small, limited to genuinely near-expiry stock, and dated.
  • The simulator is mine. Its numbers depend on my assumptions. Its conclusions about how the changes interact (capture first, block as backup, markdowns to pay for it) are what matter.
