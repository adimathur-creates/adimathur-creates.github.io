One flagship, done properly

Synthio sells a new kind of colleague to pharma: an AI medical expert that talks to doctors. Buyers have to hear it to believe it, and the people who need to hear it are in a small number of rooms each year. With a seed-stage team and limited founder time, the question isn’t which events to attend. It’s which one to own, and how to make it turn into pilots. This is how I’d run that, using Medical Affairs’ biggest US meeting as the example.

Aditya Mathur · adityamathur2004@gmail.com · all dates and figures sourced at the bottom; anything I guessed is labelled and editable

The short version

  1. Own one room, attend two. MAPS Global Annual Meeting (Miami, 14–17 March 2027) is the flagship: it’s the Medical Affairs society, and Synthio’s AI medical expert is a Medical Affairs product first. Pharma USA (Philadelphia, March 2027) and DIA (San Diego, 28 June–1 July 2027) are for meetings, not booths.
  2. Make them hear it. The product is a voice. The centre of the event is a live “call the AI medical expert” demo on a public FDA label, plus a 20-seat founder dinner for heads of Medical Affairs and Medical Information.
  3. Tell the story without logos. Pharma customers rarely let vendors name them. The campaign runs on anonymised proof (“a top-10 pharma”), the public Soteri Skin result (5,000+ eczema patients reached in 48 hours), and the live demo.
  4. Judge it on pilots, not badge scans. The calculator below shows the meetings and pilots the event must produce to pay back. If the plan can’t show that before money is spent, it gets cut.

2 · The flagship plan

MAPS 2027, from today to 30 days after

Weeks 1–2 (now)

Decide and budget. Confirm with the founders what’s already committed. Get the real exhibit and sponsorship quotes, and check if any speaking or session slots are still open. Set the pilot target that would justify the spend (see calculator).

Weeks 3–6

Build the target list. About 150 accounts: top-50 pharma plus biotechs with 2027 launches. Name 2–3 people per account: VP or Head of Medical Affairs, Head of Medical Information, Field Medical Excellence lead. Sources: the MAPS speaker list, LinkedIn, and launch calendars.

Weeks 6–10

Make the assets. Three of them: a one-page brief, “The physicians your field team never reaches”; a 90-second demo recording; and the live demo script, built on one public FDA label, on-label only, no patient data, cleared with Synthio’s compliance lead.

Weeks 10–16

Book the meetings before the flight. Founder-signed invitations to the 20-seat dinner, with a three-touch email and LinkedIn sequence to the rest of the list. Target 25 meetings booked before arrival, tracked in one sheet per account.

Event week

Run the room. Every visitor can call the AI medical expert and ask a real clinical question; every meeting ends with a named next step. Daily 15-minute debrief: who came, what they asked, which objections came up.

Within 48 hours

Follow up while they remember. A personal note from the founder who met them, with the recording of their own demo call, or a one-page pilot outline for warm accounts.

Day 30

Report to the founders. Meetings held, pilots in discussion, cost per meeting, which story landed, and whether to repeat it in 2028.

4 · The weekly rhythm

“A founder’s ten-item list becomes ten outcomes by Friday”

The same discipline runs everything else. Each item has one owner, a finished state anyone can check, and a date. Anything slipping gets raised on Wednesday, not reported on Friday.

5 · What I don’t know

  • Which events Synthio has already committed to, and the real budget. The plan bends to both.
  • What Synthio can say publicly about its top-10 pharma pilots. That decides how much of the story is case study and how much is live demo.
  • Which product the founders want to lead with in 2027. If it’s Ather for commercial teams, Pharma USA becomes the flagship and MAPS drops to meetings.
