Four shots

Under NPCI rules, a UPI AutoPay mandate gets one attempt and three retries each cycle, all outside peak hours. Spend them on the wrong days and the merchant chases the customer by hand, and the customer gets failure messages until they revoke the mandate. This proposal schedules the four attempts for when payers actually have money.

Aditya MathurBengaluruNovember 2026Code and 9 tests

Independent work. Not affiliated with or reviewed by Jodo, and no Jodo data. Payers are simulated: income patterns, spending, failure and revocation rates are labelled assumptions in the code. I don't know how Collect schedules retries today; the "daily" and "spread" arms are common patterns, not a description of Collect.

The short version

  • Why it matters to Collect. Jodo launched Collect in September 2025 after concluding its problems were recurring-payment problems: missed EMIs, failed mandates, manual reminders and reconciliation chaos [source]. Its early users include microfinance, co-operative societies and small businesses running collections on WhatsApp [source].
  • The constraint. Each mandate now gets one execution attempt and at most three retries, all in non-peak hours: outside 10 am–1 pm and 5–9:30 pm [source].
  • The cost of getting it wrong. UPI AutoPay revocations reached about 20 million a month, tied to low customer balances [source]. Every failed attempt is a wasted shot and another failure message to the customer.
  • The result (simulated, 30,000 payers, 3 months, 5 runs). Retrying every morning for three days collected 73.5% of cycles. Scheduling attempts in the window after a payer's money usually lands collected 94.1%, with 45% fewer revoked mandates.
  • The finding I didn't expect. Timing did 93% of the work. Reminders and pay links added only 1.5 points on top. The scheduler is the product; the WhatsApp nudges are polish.

1The problem

What a Collect merchant lives with

  • A coaching centre, a co-operative society or a microfinance lender sets fees or EMIs due on a fixed date, often the 1st, 5th or 10th.
  • Their customers are paid on a different day, or weekly, or irregularly. A debit on the due date often hits an empty account.
  • When the retries run out, the merchant is back to calling and messaging people on WhatsApp, the exact work Collect is meant to remove.

Why the rules raise the stakes

  • Four attempts per cycle, all off-peak [source]. A retry that lands before payday is a lost shot.
  • Debits scheduled during peak hours now risk technical declines [source].
  • Mandate executions more than doubled to 808 million in July 2025 [source]. As volume grows, so does the cost of wasted attempts.

Problem statement. For each mandate cycle, choose the day and the off-peak window for each of the four attempts so the money is most likely there. Add a reminder and a payer-initiated fallback only where they help. Measure success as cycles collected, days late, failed attempts per cycle and mandates revoked.

What the numbers say

  • Retrying every morning is the worst use of four shots. All four land before payday for anyone paid after the due date.
  • Spreading helps salaried payers but hurts weekly earners, whose money comes and goes within days.
  • Inflow-aware timing fixes both, and an afternoon window on money-in day catches income credited that morning.

What it doesn't fix

  • Irregular earners stay hardest (91% at best). There's no pattern to learn, so the fallback has to be the pay link and the merchant.
  • If the income day is learned wrong (a job change, a moved salary date), timing misses until history catches up.
  • Timing can't create money. It only stops wasting attempts.

5How I'd take it from here

Questions for 10 merchants (week 1)

  1. When are most of your customers paid: monthly, weekly or daily?
  2. Which day do you set as the due date, and why that day?
  3. What do you do when an AutoPay fails? How long does chasing take each month?
  4. Have customers complained about failure messages, or cancelled AutoPay after one?
  5. Would you let Collect move the debit a few days for a customer, if more got paid?

Metrics and test (weeks 2–6)

  • Funnel: mandate link sent → approved → first debit paid → still active at month 3.
  • Primary: cycles collected within 15 days.
  • Guardrails: mandates revoked, failed attempts per cycle, days late, merchant complaints.
  • Test: randomise by payer within willing merchants. Start with salaried payers, where history is clearest. Read collection at cycle 1 and revocations at cycle 3.

6Method and limits

  • Daily balance simulation (src/sim.js). Income lands around noon: morning attempts see the balance before it, afternoon attempts after it. Spending drains a share of the balance each day. Technical declines at 2% per attempt. Each failed attempt adds a 1.5% chance the payer revokes the mandate.
  • The reminder turns a near-miss (at least half the amount in the account) into a payment 15% of the time. The pay link is paid 50% of the time once money arrives. Both are assumptions, and they matter less than timing in every run.
  • Not modelled: bank-specific technical declines, pre-debit notification timing rules, mandates above ₹15,000 that need authentication, and merchant-chosen due dates that already match paydays.
  • Some reports say a mandate is cancelled if all four attempts fail [source]. That would make wasted attempts even costlier; I didn't model it.
