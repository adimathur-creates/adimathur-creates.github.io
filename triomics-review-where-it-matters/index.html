Review where it matters

Once AI drafts the cancer registry abstract, the registrar’s job becomes review, and review time becomes the bottleneck. Check every field and the time saved disappears. Trust the AI’s confidence and the errors it’s overconfident about reach the national cancer database. I built a review queue that spends registrar hours where fields are most likely wrong and matter most. It learns how far to trust the AI from registrars’ own edits, and keeps a random audit sample for whatever it skips.

The short version, for the sample registry

    1 · The problem

    AI moves the registrar’s work from typing to checking

    Health systems such as Yale New Haven are moving to AI-drafted abstracts that registrars review and finalise. That is the right design, but it creates a new product question: which fields deserve a registrar’s minutes? The answer has hard edges:

    The rules registries work to

    Commission on Cancer accredited programs submit new and updated cases at least once a quarter (Standard 6.4), send complete data that meets the national database’s quality checks (6.2, 6.3), and review the accuracy of at least 10% of the year’s cases, up to 200 (6.1).

    Why confidence isn’t enough

    Model confidence is rarely equally reliable across fields. A primary site read from a pathology report is easy; a first-treatment date pieced together from outside records is not. If the AI is overconfident on treatment dates, a queue that trusts its score will skip exactly the fields that are wrong most often.

    Computing…

    CSV template

    Checked, honestly. 15 automated tests on 20 synthetic registries. The calibrated queue beats reviewing in order at every budget and every seed, by 1.3–1.6x. It beats ranking by AI confidence on every seed when time is scarce (20–25% of a full review) and by a median 8% at 33%, roughly what remains after a 67% cut in review time. Above 40% the two are about even. Calibration flags every planted overconfident field, with false flags within chance (1 in 100). The errors, weights and minutes are invented; real numbers would come from Harmony’s own edit logs.

    3 · What I would ship

    A field-level review queue inside Harmony

    1. Rank fields, not cases

      Order the registrar’s work by estimated error × how much the field matters × whether the case is due this quarter. Show why each field is near the top: sources disagree, the AI is overconfident on this field, high-impact item.

    2. Learn trust from edits

      Every registrar edit is a label. Recalibrate per field and confidence band each month. Where the AI is consistently overconfident (treatment dates from outside records, in the sample), flag it for the model team, not just the queue.

    3. Keep a random audit

      Draw the CoC 6.1 quality-control sample at random and report its error rate. It measures what the queue skipped, and it’s the number a cancer committee should trust.

    4. Let registrars set the rules

      Whether a case can be submitted with unreviewed low-risk fields is a registry policy question. Make it a setting per site, decided with the registry manager.

    4 · How I would research it

    • Shadowing: two days with registrars at a customer site, timing review per field type and noting where they distrust the draft.
    • Interviews: 6–8 registrars and 2–3 registry managers. Ask which errors cost the most rework, how they handle deadlines, and what they would never let a queue skip.
    • System data: Harmony’s edit logs to replace every assumption here: real error rates by field, review minutes and calibration gaps.
    • Survey: registrars across sites on trust in AI drafts, before and after the queue, to see whether trust follows accuracy.

    5 · How we would know

    Main measure

    Weighted errors caught per registrar hour, and the error rate in the random QC sample. Together they show whether hours moved to where errors are.

    Throughput

    Registrar minutes per case, and the share of due cases submitted by each quarterly deadline.

    Model feedback

    Calibration gap per field, month on month. A gap that shrinks means the model team acted on what the queue found.

    Guardrail

    National database rejections and quality-check failures must not rise. If they do, the queue is skipping something it shouldn’t.

    6 · What I don’t know

    • How Harmony orders review today. It may already rank fields; this is how I would structure and measure it.
    • Real error rates and review minutes per field. Every weight here is a placeholder for Harmony’s edit logs.
    • Site rules on partial submission and how each state registry’s deadlines interact with the quarterly CoC cycle.

    7 · How it’s built

    Last quarter’s registrar edits→error rate by field, confidence band, conflict→rank this quarter’s fields by risk × impact per minute→fill the registrar hours→random QC sample for the rest
