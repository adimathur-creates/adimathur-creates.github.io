Checkout events partners can trust

A platform spec and a working simulator for one webhook contract that works the same on every checkout setup: one documented payload, signed, retried, deduplicated, and visible in a delivery log.

The short version

  • The problem (from public material): a partner app that wants checkout events gets a different answer depending on which checkout the merchant runs: a webhook that someone on the provider's team has to add, an event from the store platform, or nothing. The public setup guide does not document the payload, retries, signing or limits.
  • What I'd build: five events in one versioned envelope, published the same way by every checkout setup, with signed requests, 8 delivery tries over 24 hours, a dead-letter queue with replay, stable event IDs, self-serve subscriptions and a delivery log.
  • What the simulation shows (fictional): across 93,254 subscribed deliveries (40 merchants, 5 partner apps, 30 days), the share that arrives usable within 5 minutes goes from 73.1% to 99.7%. Silent losses go from 4,060 to 0, and partners doing the same thing twice (two WhatsApp messages, two shipments) from 719 to 0.
  • How I'd know it worked: one north-star metric (share of subscribed events delivered usable within 5 minutes), plus adoption (share of integrations on the contract, share set up without a ticket) and reliability (first-try success, p95 latency, dead-letter age).

1. The problem, from public material

  • GoKwik's own help article on setting up its abandoned-cart webhook covers the URL and the trigger stage, but does not document the payload, retry behaviour, authentication or limits, and mentions both "real time" and a 24-48 hour wait. Source
  • A partner's help doc explains that on the one-click modal checkout (their "Version 1"), merchants usually cannot add webhooks themselves; the merchant shares a URL and GoKwik's Merchant Integration team adds it. It also says abandoned checkouts from that setup no longer appear in Shopify automatically, so the custom webhook is now needed. Source
  • On the Shopify-native blocks setup ("Version 2"), the same partner picks events up from Shopify instead: a second integration path for the same job. Source
  • Other partners' docs start with "contact your GoKwik POC" or "share this webhook URL with your POC". Source
  • In December 2025 a Shopify app developer asked publicly whether GoKwik offers any API, webhook or event stream, and how to carry UTM parameters through checkout, because their app could not see journey data for GoKwik merchants. The thread had no reply. Source
  • GoKwik's platform product postings talk about owning shared services, APIs and data contracts, standardising integrations across Shopify, custom storefronts and other platforms, and measuring adoption and reliability. This page is one concrete slice of that. Source

Problem statement. Checkout events reach partner apps in a different way, in a different shape and with different reliability depending on the merchant's checkout setup, and nobody (merchant, partner or provider) can see whether a given event arrived. So every integration is a one-off, failures are silent, and integration work grows with every new merchant and partner.

This is my reading of public pages. I have not seen GoKwik's internal systems, volumes or failure rates, and some of this may already be solved. The first thing I would do in the role is check it (see section 9).

What to build first

  
    
    
  1. One envelope, five versioned events: checkout.abandoned, order.placed, payment.captured, order.cod_confirmed, order.cancelled. Same fields on every checkout setup.
    2. 
    
  2. Delivery service: HMAC-SHA256 signed requests with a timestamp; 8 tries over 24 hours (1 min, 5 min, 30 min, 2 h, 6 h, 12 h, 24 h after the first); then a dead-letter queue with replay.
    3. 
    
  3. Stable event IDs: the same checkout in the same state always gets the same ID, so re-sends and retries can be ignored safely.
    4. 
    
  4. Self-serve subscriptions: in the merchant dashboard and through a partner API, with a "send test event" button.
    5. 
    
  5. Delivery log: per merchant and partner, 30 days: status, attempts, last response code; alerts when an endpoint drops below 95% success in an hour.

User stories and acceptance criteria

  
US1 · Partner developer · P0
As a partner developer, I want one documented payload for each event on every checkout setup, so that I build my integration once.
Given merchants on the modal, platform-native and custom storefront setups, when a checkout with a captured phone is abandoned, then each sends checkout.abandoned in version 2026-11-01 with amount_paise as a whole number, phone_e164 and a UTC created_at; 100% of events sampled in staging pass the contract checker.

  
US2 · Partner developer · P0
As a partner developer, I want failed deliveries retried with the same ID, so that a short outage on my side does not lose events.
Given my endpoint times out (10 s) or returns 5xx, then the event is retried at 1 min, 5 min, 30 min, 2 h, 6 h, 12 h and 24 h with the same id; after the last failure it moves to the dead-letter queue, shows in both delivery logs, and a replay sends the original id.

  
US3 · Partner developer · P0
As a partner developer, I want to verify that a request really came from the checkout provider.
Every request carries t and v1 (HMAC-SHA256 of t.body); the guide tells receivers to reject anything older than 5 minutes; during secret rotation both secrets work for 24 hours.

  
US4 · Merchant ops · P0
As a merchant admin, I want to connect an app's endpoint myself, so that I do not wait on a ticket.
Given the admin role, when I add an endpoint and pick events, then a test event is sent and its result shown within 10 seconds, no integration-team action is needed, and the audit log records who changed what.

  
US5 · Merchant ops and integration team · P1
As a merchant or support agent, I want to see whether events reached an app, so that I can fix problems before customers notice.
The delivery log filters by partner, event type and status, shows attempts and last response code, and alerts the merchant and partner when an endpoint's success rate falls below 95% over an hour (at least 20 attempts).

  
US6 · Internal product team · P1
As an internal product team, I want to consume the same events, so that the platform has one source of truth.
Internal consumers subscribe through the same catalogue; a change that removes, renames or retypes a field fails the contract tests in CI until a new version is published.

  
US7 · Partner developer · P1
As a shipping app, I want the COD confirmation event from every setup, including the platform-native one.
On the platform-native setup, order.cod_confirmed appears in the delivery log within 5 minutes at p95. Depends on whether checkout blocks can publish this event; open question.

8. Risks and open questions

  
    
    
  • Platform-native setup: can checkout blocks publish provider-only events like COD confirmation reliably within the store platform's rules? If not, US7 drops and partners keep two paths for that setup.
    • 
    
  • Customer data: payloads carry phone numbers. Send only to apps the merchant enabled, only the fields each event needs, and get a legal review against India's data protection law (DPDP Act) before launch.
    • 
    
  • Sale-day load: publishing must not slow checkout. Events go to a queue after the order is saved, never in the checkout request itself.
    • 
    
  • Partner migration: partners with working one-off integrations have no reason to move. Dual-send and a long sunset lower the cost; the delivery log is the reason to move.
    • 
    
  • Ownership: who owns the event catalogue when a product team needs a new field? Proposal: product teams propose, platform reviews, the contract tests enforce.
    •
