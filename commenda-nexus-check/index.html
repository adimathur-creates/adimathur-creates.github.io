The problem

Who has it

Indian and other foreign SaaS companies selling to US customers through Stripe, with no US office. Since Wayfair (2018), selling enough into a state creates a duty to collect its sales tax. States apply this to sellers based abroad too.

Why it breaks

45 states and DC each set their own threshold, transaction test, look-back window and rule on what counts. Four dropped their transaction test between Jan 2025 and Aug 2026. A raw export carries failed and test charges, refunds, INR settlement amounts, typo'd states and blank addresses. Any one of these can flip a verdict.

Where it hurts Commenda

Commenda offers prospects a free nexus analysis and promises alerts before a threshold is crossed. Each analysis means cleaning someone's export by hand. Slow analyses lose deals. A wrong "you're fine" is worse: it is the customer's tax bill and Commenda's credibility.

I don't know how Commenda's team runs these analyses internally. The free analysis and the alerts are on Commenda's indirect tax page. The workflow pains are what I'd expect from my year handling Stripe transaction data, and the first thing I'd check by sitting with the team.

"Est. uncollected tax" = taxable sales from the crossing date × the state's average combined rate (Texas at 80% of the charge, Connecticut at the 1% business rate). STEP 4

What a person must review

The tool runs every state three ways: the best reading of the data, refunds not netted, and missing states spread by known sales. If the verdict holds in all three, it is firm. If it changes, it is fragile and goes to an analyst with the reason. Analysts spend their time on a handful of states, not on all 47 jurisdictions.

The product thinking

Problem statement
A finance lead at a cross-border SaaS company can't tell from their payments data which US states they owe sales tax in, since when, or how much. Today, getting that answer means a person cleaning an export by hand, and the answer is only as good as their cleaning.
Who uses this
First, Commenda's indirect tax analysts running free nexus analyses for prospects. Later, customers themselves, as the "before you cross" alert inside the product.
Outcome
Exposure report in minutes, not hours, with every verdict traceable to a rule, a source and a date.

Four decisions I made, and why

  1. Clean before you count. The most common errors are in the data, not the rules: summing INR settlement amounts as dollars, counting failed and test charges, missing "Calif." when you filter on "CA". The tool shows each fix so an analyst can challenge it.
  2. Rules are data with a source and a date. Every threshold carries its effective dates. Utah's 200-transaction test counts for sales before 1 Jul 2025 and not after. When a state changes a rule, one row changes, and the self-checks catch regressions.
  3. Firm vs fragile. The tool doesn't hide uncertainty. It runs the data three ways and sends only the verdicts that change to a person. This is the same "stage it for human review" idea Commenda describes for its agents.
  4. It never files. It recommends and explains. A person signs off before anything reaches a customer or a state.

How I'd measure it

  • Time to first exposure report per prospect. I'd measure today's baseline in week one rather than guess it.
  • Share of state verdicts that come out firm, which shows how much analyst time is saved.
  • Analyst override rate on firm verdicts. If it rises above a few percent, the rules or the clean-up are wrong, and that matters more than speed.
  • Free analysis → paid indirect tax customer conversion, compared against analyses done the old way.
  • Guardrail: missed thresholds. Any customer who crossed before we told them is a serious incident.

My first two weeks with this at Commenda

  1. Days 1–3: sit with the people running nexus analyses. Do five real ones by hand alongside them. Time each step and log every manual fix.
  2. Days 4–7: run this engine on ten past analyses. Every verdict that differs from the human one is either a bug in my rules or a miss in the old process. Both are worth knowing.
  3. Week 2: put it in the analysts' hands behind a review step. Add mappings for Shopify, Chargebee and NetSuite exports, in the order the team actually receives them.

What it doesn't do yet

  • Marketplace sales, which many states exclude from the seller's own threshold.
  • Physical nexus from US employees or contractors. This links to Commenda's entity vs EOR work, since hiring in a state can create nexus on day one.
  • Exemption and resale certificates, local-only taxes (Chicago, Colorado home-rule cities, Alaska) and look-back periods for voluntary disclosure.
  • EU and UK VAT. The same engine shape fits the EU's €10,000 OSS threshold; only the rules table changes.
