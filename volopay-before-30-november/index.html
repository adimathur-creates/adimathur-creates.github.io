Before 30 November

Volopay already moves the money for Indian companies’ cards, bills and claims. Whether that spend comes back as GST credit depends on two things decided at the moment of purchase, and one deadline. For FY 2025-26 that deadline is four weeks away. I built the check that finds the money, and the case for building it into the product.

The short version

    1 · The working check

    Upload a spend export and a GSTR-2B, get the money back

    It loads with a fictional Bengaluru company: a full year of card, bill-pay and reimbursement spend, plus the GSTR-2B its suppliers’ returns produced. Swap in your own two CSVs and it runs the same way, in the browser. Nothing is uploaded anywhere.

    What this is not. It does not file returns, act in the GST portal or give tax advice. Every action is a recommendation for the finance team or their CA to confirm. Matching was 100% correct on 3,563 filed invoices across 20 synthetic years, but I wrote that noise myself; real exports will be messier, which is why ambiguous matches go to a person instead of being guessed.

    2 · Why this problem

    GST credit is cash, and it has three ways to leak

    A company can offset the GST it paid on business purchases against the GST it owes. On most spend that is 18% of the bill. The law attaches conditions, and each one is a place the money falls out:

    1 · The invoice must carry our GSTIN

    A card charge or a claim paid without the company GSTIN on the invoice is a B2C sale. No credit. This is decided at the moment of purchase, when the employee checks out, which is exactly where Volopay sits.

    2 · The supplier must report it

    Credit is allowed only if the invoice appears in the buyer’s auto-drafted GSTR-2B (s.16(2)(aa)), which is built from suppliers’ GSTR-1 filings. A late or careless vendor means no credit, even though we paid them.

    3 · It must be claimed in time

    FY 2025-26 credit cannot be taken after 30 November 2026 (s.16(4)). For a monthly filer, the vendor’s October GSTR-1 (due 11 November) feeds the last GSTR-3B that can still carry it. After that the credit is gone.

    And the reverse risk

    Some credit must not be claimed: food, cabs, club memberships and gifts are blocked by s.17(5). Since late 2024, buyers also accept, reject or hold each supplier invoice in the Invoice Management System (IMS) before it reaches GSTR-2B. Claiming the wrong thing costs interest and penalties.

    Why Volopay, specifically. Volopay already has the parts: cards and UPI virtual cards, bill pay with maker-checker approvals, reimbursements, a native Tally integration, and an AI receipt scan that reads and matches receipts to transactions. Its India customers include Rapido, Porter, Urban Company and Pocket FM. The receipts it already reads contain the supplier GSTIN, our GSTIN and the invoice number, which are the three keys this check needs. From the public pages I could read, none of Volopay’s India pages describe a GST-credit check. If one exists internally, this page is a second opinion on how to scope it.

      5 · How it works

      Five passes, each one explainable to a CA

      1. Read and clean. Map columns by name (it recognises the portal’s “GSTIN of supplier”, “Invoice Value(₹)” headers and skips the title rows above them), parse Indian number formats and three date styles, and validate every GSTIN with its check character.
      2. Match. Pass 1: same supplier GSTIN and invoice number after removing separators and leading zeros (“BSM/0042/25-26” = “BSM-42-25-26”). Pass 2: same GSTIN, value within 1% or ₹10, date within 5 days, exactly one candidate. Pass 3: same invoice number and value under any GSTIN, which catches a wrong GSTIN in the vendor master. No 2B row is used twice.
      3. Classify. Blocked categories (s.17(5)) and non-GST spend first, then imports of services (reverse charge), then matched rows (claimable, value differs, not available for place of supply), then unmatched rows (no GSTIN, invalid GSTIN, vendor has not reported).
      4. Price. Matched rows use the tax in GSTR-2B. Unmatched rows estimate tax from the rate on the row (default 18%) and are labelled as estimates.
      5. Act. A chase list per vendor (most money first, nearest deadline first), a fix list per merchant, and an IMS action for every 2B row: accept, keep pending, or find the owner.
      Volopay export (cards, bills, claims)+GSTR-2B B2B sheet→engine.js, in the browser→chase list · merchant fixes · IMS actions · CSVs

      20 automated tests cover GSTIN validation against the GST portal’s sample, matching accuracy against known answers, the s.17(5) guardrail, the 30 November cut-off, messy input and the vendor message. In production, the export would come from Volopay’s ledger, and 2B and IMS through a GSP.

      6 · Rollout and how we know it worked

      Weeks 1–4 (before 30 Nov)

      • Customer success runs it for 5 India customers with the most bill-pay volume
      • Each gets the chase list and merchant fixes in a 30-minute call
      • Re-run on the next GSTR-2B to count what came back

      What decides the next step

      • Primary: ₹ of credit that appears in 2B after the chase, per customer
      • Speed: minutes from files to chase list
      • Leak closed: share of card spend carrying our GSTIN, 60 days later
      • Guardrail: zero blocked credits marked claimable

      Kill or continue. If, across five customers, recovered credit does not clearly exceed what the check costs in CSM time, stop and keep only the merchant-fix list as an onboarding checklist. If it does, build the point-of-spend GSTIN capture first, because it prevents the loss instead of chasing it.

      7 · What I would ask the first five customers

      1. Who reconciles GSTR-2B today, how long does it take, and does it include card and reimbursement spend or only vendor bills?
      2. When an employee pays on a card, how do you get the GST invoice in the company name? How often does it not happen?
      3. What did you do with IMS last month: accept everything, or check each invoice?
      4. Which vendors do you chase every quarter, and how?
      5. If Volopay told you the ₹ you are about to lose and wrote the vendor emails, who in your team would act on it?

      8 · Edge cases handled, and the ones that are not

      Handled

      • Invoice numbers with different separators or leading zeros
      • Supplier dates a day off; values off by rounding
      • Wrong GSTIN in the vendor master
      • Two possible 2B rows: sent to a person, not guessed
      • Out-of-state hotels marked not available in 2B
      • Foreign SaaS: reverse charge, so no 2B row is expected
      • 2B invoices with no matching spend: held as pending, never auto-accepted

      Not yet

      • Credit notes and amendments (B2BA, CDNR sheets)
      • Multiple company GSTINs (one per state)
      • Split payments and one invoice paid by two cards
      • The 180-day payment rule (low risk here: card and bill pay settle the vendor at once)
      • Partial business use (s.17(1)/(2) apportionment)
