Why it looks like this

  1. 1Filling a form isn't the same as wanting a surprise call. "Later" and "WhatsApp only" keep the lead instead of burning it with an unwanted call.
  2. 2The AI is disclosed three times: here, on WhatsApp, and in the first line of the call. This is my answer to the founder's "nobody should know it's a bot".
  3. 3A heads-up with the exact number, so an unknown 080 number isn't ignored as spam. The two buttons are the off-ramps.
  4. 4A price question gets a choice: a consult, a callback with a price range, or both. The assistant never quotes a price, and it doesn't push the booking past the question.
  5. 5Only two real slots, pulled live from the clinic calendar. It reads the booking back and books only after a clear yes. If the calendar doesn't answer, it never says "booked": the case goes to staff as pending.
  6. 6The reminders do the no-show work. My success metric is visits completed, not bookings made.

Why it looks like this

  1. AUrgent first, then by the time we promised the patient. Callbacks are only offered inside staffed hours and each has a named owner. An urgent handoff nobody picks up in 5 minutes moves to the next person.
  2. BEvery handoff shows the patient's own words with a timestamp, and the summary may only restate what was said. The model writes for a person here, so it must not add a budget or a preference nobody mentioned.
  3. CA booking only counts if the calendar returned a reference. The assistant can't "say" a booking into existence.
  4. DClashes and unconfirmed bookings are shown, not hidden. If the calendar times out, the assistant doesn't retry blindly or tell the patient "booked". Staff check it first.
  5. EThe desk is measured on callbacks done on time, which they control. Consult-to-treatment stays a clinic number, since the dentist, price and case mix drive it.
  6. ·There's no "calls made" counter anywhere on this screen. That's deliberate: it rises whether or not anyone walks in.
