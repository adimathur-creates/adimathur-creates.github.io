Before the Square-off

Margin trading is Groww's fastest-growing business, and most new users can't see how close they are to a forced sale. Show every position its distance to a margin call, warn in time, and test whether fewer forced sales keep people trading longer.

Aditya Mathur · October 2026 · Code · Also for Groww: Where's My Money

Read this first
  • I don't work at Groww and used no internal data. The margin rules come from Groww's public MTF terms and risk policy. The business numbers come from Groww's results as reported in the press.
  • The rules leave one thing undefined: how the "collateral to loan" ratio is calculated. I used the standard reading, equity divided by required margin, and the page says so wherever it matters.
  • Every position and price in sections 5 and 6 is synthetic. Customer behaviour in section 6 is a set of assumptions you can change, not a finding.
  • The phone card below is my concept, not Groww's app.

Most of Groww's MTF users are new to leverage: the number roughly tripled in a year. The terms they accept say four things most of them have never had to deal with:

  • A margin call comes when cover falls to 85%, and an app notification counts as the call.
  • The shortfall must be paid by the end of the next trading day. Otherwise Groww sells, by T+4.
  • If 80% of the margin is wiped out, every open position is sold at once, without notice.
  • Shares bought on margin must be pledged on the day of purchase. If the pledge isn't approved by the cut-off, the shares are sold the next day.
"MTF is one area where the business growth is scaring me."Nithin Kamath, Zerodha, quoted by Inc42. Groww tightened its own MTF and intraday limits after the March-quarter volatility.

A forced sale is bad for everyone. The customer sells at the worst moment and often blames the app, Groww answers the complaint, and any shortfall sits on Groww's own lending book. My bet: a customer who understands their margin before a call stays in MTF longer than one who learns about it from a forced sale. That is the claim worth testing, because it is where helping customers and growing MTF income point the same way.

The same numbers for every haircut

Haircut is the share of a position the user must pay for, set per stock by exchange risk rules (VaR + ELM) and Groww. 25% is 4x leverage. "Days to a call at a flat price" assumes interest is left unpaid and added to the loan, which the terms allow.

3Five ways an MTF user gets sold out

Each one comes from a specific line in the public terms. Each has a different fix, and one can't be warned about at all.

1
Pledge not done on the dayBought on margin, never finished the pledge. Risk policy: not approved by the cut-off on T → sold on T+1.
Fix: make the pledge part of the buy, with a countdown on the confirmation screen and a reminder two hours before the cut-off. This is a flow problem, not a market problem, so it should go close to zero.
2
A normal day's fallAt 4x, a 4.8% fall is a margin call. Mid caps move that much in an ordinary week.Call at cover ≤ 85%; top up by T+1.
Fix: show the call price on the order screen, and alert at 2.5% away (section 5) with the exact rupees or shares that fix it.
3
The haircut goes upThe exchange or Groww raises a stock's haircut. A 25% → 35% change triggers a call with no price move.Clause 7: the broker can revise margin rates at any time.
Fix: when a haircut changes, tell each affected holder what it means for their position, before the next session.
4
Interest driftAt 4x with interest unpaid, a stock that never moves still reaches a call in about four months.Clause 12a: interest accrues daily, and unpaid interest can join the loan.
Fix: show interest paid so far and the break-even price on every position, and suggest paying interest before it eats the margin.
5
A gap downA 20% fall at 4x triggers an instant square-off. Overnight news can do that before any alert can help.Risk policy: 80% erosion → all positions sold, no notice.
Fix: none after the fact. The only lever is at the order: don't default the slider to maximum leverage, and show what a 20% gap would do.

4What I'd build

At the order
  • Call price and square-off price, before the buy button.
  • Leverage slider that opens at a suggested level, not the maximum.
  • The pledge step built into the buy, with its deadline.
On the position
  • A cover meter with the 85% line marked.
  • Distance to a call in % and rupees.
  • Interest so far and today's break-even price.
When it gets close
  • Alert at 2.5% from a call, once per episode, not every day.
  • The exact fix: "add ₹3,675" or "sell 16 shares".
  • A list for the risk team of positions likely to get a call tomorrow, so they can staff support for it.

5Where to draw the alert line

Alert too early and people learn to ignore it. Alert too late and there's no time to act. I ran 4,000 synthetic positions over a 60-day market path with one shock day and measured both sides.

    7How I'd test it

    Design
    • Randomise new MTF users 50/50 for 8 weeks; existing users keep the current app.
    • Treatment gets all three surfaces from section 4.
    • Pre-register the metrics below before launch.
    Primary metric
    • Forced sales per 1,000 MTF users: unmet calls, instant square-offs and pledge sales.
    • Secondary: share of calls met; complaints that mention a square-off.
    Guardrails
    • MTF book per user and interest income per user. Some drop is expected; agree the limit with the business first.
    • 60-day MTF retention: the number that decides whether this pays for itself.

    The primary metric in SQL

    -- forced sales per 1,000 MTF users, by experiment arm
with users as (
  select a.user_id, a.arm
  from experiment_assignment a
  where a.experiment = 'mtf_margin_health'
    and a.assigned_at between :start and :end
),
forced as (
  select p.user_id, count(*) as n
  from mtf_positions p
  join users u using (user_id)
  where p.close_reason in ('margin_call_liquidation', 'mtm_square_off', 'pledge_not_approved')
    and p.closed_at < :end
  group by p.user_id
)
select u.arm,
       count(*)                                  as users,
       coalesce(sum(f.n), 0)                     as forced_sales,
       1000.0 * coalesce(sum(f.n), 0) / count(*) as per_1000_users
from users u
left join forced f using (user_id)
group by u.arm;

    Table and column names are mine. In week one I'd map them to Groww's real tables and check that "close reason" is actually recorded. If it isn't, logging it is the first ticket.

    9What would prove me wrong

    • Groww may already show most of this. I only have the public terms and help articles, not the live app. If the call price is already on every position, the gap is in alerts and the order screen, not the card.
    • Fewer calls may mean less MTF income. If warned users trim positions and don't come back, the guardrail catches it, and the honest result is a trade-off for the business to decide, not a win to claim.
    • The bet assumes forced sales cause churn. If users who get sold out return at the same rate as everyone else, the retention case falls apart. The test answers this directly.
    • My cover formula may not be Groww's. The public policy doesn't define the ratio. If the internal formula differs, the thresholds move, but the product doesn't change.
    • The interest rate isn't consistent in public: the MTF page advertises 14.95% a year, and the terms PDF lists 15.75% under ₹25 lakh. I used 14.95%. The engine takes either.
