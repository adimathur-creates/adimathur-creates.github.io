Settlement Leak Finder

A finance team already has three files: its orders, the gateway's settlement report and the bank statement. This reconciles them in one pass, shows which rule closed each match, checks every fee against the contract, and turns what is left into a claim the gateway can act on.

orders.csv↔gateway_settlement.csv↔bank_statement.csv

The problem

Who has it

Finance and ops teams at Indian merchants taking lakhs of online payments a month: D2C brands, gaming apps, e-pharmacies, insurers. Each payment touches three systems that never agree on IDs, timing or amounts.

Why it breaks

Gateways settle in daily batches, net of fees, GST, refunds and chargebacks. Bank narrations drop the UTR. Debit cards get billed at credit card rates. Orders marked "failed" still capture money. Matching on one key leaves a pile of false alarms that hide the real losses.

Why it matters to Osfin

Osfin already sells this outcome: PG charge auditing for Games24x7, settlement visibility for PharmEasy, AI exception triage. The hard product question is which exceptions to auto-close, which to escalate, and how to turn a recon result into money recovered.

I don't know how Osfin's modules work inside. The customer use cases above are from Osfin's Payments & Cards page. The failure patterns come from my time owning settlement and reconciliation for a payment gateway's acquiring product at EnKash.

PRD: Settlement leak detection

Problem
Merchants reconcile to find whether money arrived, not whether the right money arrived. Fee overbilling, short and missing settlements, and over-refunds sit in exception queues with no rupee value or owner, so nothing gets claimed back.
Users
Recon analyst, who works the queue every morning. Finance controller, who signs off month-end and owns the gateway relationship. Osfin implementation team, who configure rules for each new customer.
Goal
Every unmatched rupee has a reason, an owner and a next step within one day of the bank statement landing. Money owed by the gateway goes out as a claim without manual work.
Non-goals
Disputes and chargeback representment (separate module), GL posting, multi-currency.

Requirements

IDRequirementAcceptance criteria
P0-1Match ladder with per-rule countsEvery matched record stores the rule that matched it. The ladder shows each rule's closes, and rules can be reordered without a deploy.
P0-2Contract-aware fee auditA rate card per gateway and payment method, effective-dated. Overbilling above ₹0.05 per transaction is flagged with the rupee amount including GST.
P0-3Reason codes with owner and next stepEvery exception gets exactly one code from a fixed list. Each code maps to an owner team and a default action.
P0-4Settlement watchA batch with no bank credit after the SLA plus 2 days is "missing". A UTR-matched credit below the batch net by more than the tolerance is "short".
P1-1Claim packOne click produces the gateway claim with batch IDs, transaction IDs and totals. Its status is tracked until the money arrives.
P1-2Auto-close for known benign patternsRounding within tolerance and late-but-arrived settlements close themselves, with an audit trail. The analyst override rate is tracked for each rule.
P2-1AI-drafted claim and root-cause notesDrafts only. A person approves before anything is sent.

How I'd measure it

  • North star: rupees recovered per month from gateway claims, as a share of rupees flagged.
  • Auto-match rate by rule, from the ladder. A sudden drop in one rule usually means a gateway changed its file format.
  • Exception aging: the share of exceptions older than 7 days, by owner.
  • Guardrail: false auto-closes. Every exception that gets reopened after auto-close counts against that rule.

Go-to-market

  • Who to sell to first: merchants on two or more gateways with high card share, such as gaming, travel and e-pharmacy. Fee audits pay off most there.
  • Wedge: a free 90-day leak audit. The prospect uploads three files and gets a rupee figure and a claim pack in a day. The first claim that comes back is the sales case for the full recon subscription.
  • Buyer and champion: the CFO buys on rupees recovered; the recon lead champions on hours saved. The demo shows both.
  • Launch: five design partners from existing payments customers. Measure recovered rupees and override rate for 6 weeks, then publish one customer number.

Risks

  • Wrong contract rates create false claims and damage the merchant's gateway relationship. Rate cards need sign-off and effective dates.
  • Gateway file formats change without notice. Per-rule match rates are the early warning.
  • Too much auto-close hides real losses. Start with rounding and late settlements only, and widen based on override data.
