1. The problem

What is true today (public sources)

  • Paytm's MCP server lets merchants manage payments from Cursor, ChatGPT, Claude and n8n with natural-language prompts, and is pitched for agentic AI payments and bot-led shopping.
  • Its tools create and fetch payment links, list orders, and start and track refunds (create_link / create_payment_link, fetch_link, initiate_refund). Paytm's own example: "Create a ₹500 payment link".
  • When a link is created with a mobile number or email, it is sent to the customer by SMS or email automatically.
  • An independent review of the connector found 21 tools, 9 of which write, and sign-in scopes that only establish identity: there is no separate read versus write permission to grant.

Why that matters for Payment Links

  • An agent's mistake is not a wrong answer on a screen. It is an SMS with a payment request, from the merchant's name, to a real person. Or money leaving the account.
  • The common agent failures are small and boring: a misread "dhai hazaar", two digits swapped in a number, the same create fired twice after a timeout, a refund larger than what is left on the order, an instruction copied from a customer's email.
  • Two easy answers both fail. An amount cap misses small mistakes. Asking before every action trains merchants to tap Approve without reading.
  • Competitors are moving the same way: PhonePe launched SmartPages (June 2026), which builds a payment page from a merchant's prompt.

Problem statement. Merchants who connect an AI agent to Payment Links need it to create and manage links on its own, without a wrong amount, a wrong recipient, a duplicate or an unwanted refund ever reaching a customer or leaving their account, and without approving every single action.

1. Merchant asks"Meera ko saadhe chaar hazaar ka link bhej do"

6. PRD: Agent-safe Payment Links

Users

  • Small merchant owner who runs the business from WhatsApp and wants "link bhej do" to just work.
  • Merchant's staff using a shared agent; the owner wants limits on what staff and agent can do.
  • Developers and bot builders building shopping or collections bots on the MCP server.
  • Paytm risk and support, who pay for every wrong link in tickets and complaints.

Goals

  • Agents can create and manage links unattended for the large majority of requests.
  • No wrong-amount, wrong-recipient, duplicate or unauthorised money-out action executes without a human seeing it.
  • When the agent is refused, it gets a reason it can act on, so it fixes the call instead of retrying blindly.

Not in scope (v1)

  • Judging whether the merchant's business is legitimate (that is onboarding and transaction monitoring).
  • Voice or WhatsApp front ends; the gate sits under any front end.
  • Payouts and subscriptions (same pattern, later).

Requirements

P0

  • Scopes: merchant grants read / create link / edit link / refund separately in the dashboard. Accept: a token without refund scope gets a clear refusal on initiate_refund.
  • Decision on every write (AUTO / CONFIRM / BLOCK) with reason codes returned to the agent. Accept: all 41 test cases match; the reason text names the field to fix.
  • Approve card in the business app: amount, person, masked number, channel, expiry, single or part payment. Ignored for 10 minutes: nothing runs. Accept: card text generated from the final arguments, not from the agent's summary.
  • Idempotency: retry within 2 minutes returns the existing link; refund reference required and unique. Accept: 0 duplicate links in a retry storm test.
  • Instruction source: MCP clients mark whether a write was asked by the user or derived from tool output; derived writes are refused. Accept: injection cases P01 and P02 blocked.

P1

  • Merchant-set limits (auto up to ₹X; refunds above ₹Y need the owner, not staff).
  • "Resend existing link" as a first-class tool, so agents stop recreating.
  • Weekly digest: what the agent did, what was refused and why.
  • Hinglish and regional-number parsing shared with the voice stack.

Metrics

  • North star: agent-created links paid without the merchant correcting, cancelling or re-sending them.
  • Hard guardrail: unsafe writes executed without a human = 0 (from replay and from support tickets tagged "agent").
  • Friction: share of writes asking for a tap (target under 20%), time to approve, share of taps rejected or edited (if near 0%, the rule asking is too strict).
  • Agent recovery: share of BLOCKs followed by a corrected call within 1 minute.
  • Adoption: merchants with an agent connected who create 10+ links a week through it.

Rollout

  1. Week 1-2, shadow mode: run the gate on live MCP traffic, decide but do not enforce. Compare decisions with what happened (cancelled links, refund tickets).
  2. Week 3: enforce BLOCK only for retries, invented contacts and over-refunds (near-zero false positives).
  3. Week 4-6: turn on CONFIRM with the approve card for 5% of agent-connected merchants; A/B against shadow.
  4. Then: scopes in the dashboard and the developer docs; default on for new MCP sign-ups.

Risks

  • Too many taps: merchants switch the agent off. Watch reject rate per rule.
  • Agents loop on BLOCK: reason codes must say what to change.
  • Customer book is incomplete for new merchants: "invented contact" fires often; fall back to CONFIRM in their first 30 days.
  • Paytm may already run some of these checks server-side; I could not see tool annotations without signing in.

What I would ask the team first

  • How many merchants have the MCP server connected, and what share of its calls are writes?
  • Of agent-created links, how many are cancelled or re-sent within a day? That is the cheapest proxy for agent mistakes.
  • Do refunds through the MCP server already need a second factor?
  • Is there a link-level "created by agent" flag in the data today?
