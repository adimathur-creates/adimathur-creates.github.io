From signup to first payment: how I'd run a merchant activation program from a product leadership office

The role I'm applying for runs critical product programs, keeps leadership reviews short and useful, and builds the tracking that makes this possible. So instead of a feature idea, this is a program pack: one defined problem, structured, sized, planned across seven functions, measured, and reported in a weekly note that writes itself from the tracker.

The problem: merchants sign up for a payment gateway, then a large share never take a single successful payment, or take weeks to. Every one of them is sales and onboarding cost already spent.

1. The note I'd send the CPO (one page)

Written as if for a weekly product leadership review. Numbers are from the fictional simulation below.

Problem

Of merchants who sign up, 23.8% take a first successful payment within 14 days and 45.9% ever do by day 45. The rest stall at four points: documents never finished, KYC rework never completed, integration never live, and live with no customer payment. Each stalled merchant is acquisition and onboarding cost already spent.

Why it matters now

Since 1 January 2026, every new merchant must clear the RBI's stricter due-diligence standard, so onboarding has more steps where a merchant can stall. Activation is also where PayU's own AI work is heading next (see section 2). And products sold on top of payments, like Agent HQ, only earn on merchants who are live.

Recommendation

Run activation as one program with four levers, in this order: the KYC fix-it queue and first payment by link first (inside Product's control, largest effect), voice and WhatsApp nudges as a holdout test, and the simplified route only after Compliance decides. In the simulation, all four together move the 14-day rate from 23.8% to 39.8%, cut median days to first payment from 13.6 to 9.9, and add about 222 merchants active at day 30 for every month of signups, paying back in about 3 months on the stated assumptions.

Decisions I need

  1. Compliance: approve or reject the simplified due-diligence route for micro merchants (turnover up to ₹40 lakh), excluding high-risk categories.
  2. Finance: a mapping so payments taken by link reconcile to the same merchant ledger, before the link pilot starts.
  3. Everyone: one definition of "first successful payment" (excluding test and self-payments) that all teams report against.

What would make me stop

If merchants who went through the simplified route show a 60-day fraud or chargeback rate more than 1.5 times the standard route, the simplified route pauses for that category until Risk reviews it.

3. The problem, structured

Question: why do signed-up merchants not take a first successful payment within 14 days, and which causes can we fix in one quarter?

  • A. They never finish documents.
    • Hypothesis: friction at the bank account and website steps, not lack of intent. Test: drop-off by step; compare sales-assisted vs self-serve.
    • Hypothesis: some aren't ready (no website, no current account). Test: share with no website by path; outcome after a reminder.
  • B. KYC sends them back and they don't return.
    • Hypothesis: rejection reasons are unclear, so merchants don't know what to fix. Test: rework completion rate by reason code.
    • Hypothesis: small merchants carry more documents than the rules require. Test: share of micro merchants eligible for the simplified route but sent down the full route.
  • C. Approved, but integration never goes live.
    • Hypothesis: API merchants wait on their own developers; plugin merchants hit configuration errors. Test: time from keys issued to first test call, by path.
  • D. Live, but no customer pays.
    • Hypothesis: the store isn't launched yet, or the merchant is still testing. Test: share with test-mode activity only; sales notes.
  • E. Correctly rejected. Not a leak. Kept out of the north star so nobody is rewarded for approving risky merchants.

5. Levers and the business case

Four levers, each aimed at one stall point. Turn them on and off; the simulation re-runs on the same merchants, so differences come from the lever, not from random noise.

LeverWhat changesStall it targetsLead owner
L1 Fix-it rework queueEach KYC rejection reason gets its own fix screen (for example, "the name on your bank account doesn't match your PAN: upload a cancelled cheque or change the account"), instead of a generic "documents rejected".BProduct (Onboarding) + Risk Ops
L2 Simplified routeEligible micro merchants (turnover up to ₹40 lakh, not high-risk) go down the simplified due-diligence route the RBI allows. Checks still happen; fewer documents.A, BRisk & Compliance + Product
L3 First payment by linkOnce KYC is approved, plugin and API merchants can take their first live payment by payment link while their integration finishes.CProduct (Payment Links) + Sales
L4 Stall nudgesA Hindi/English voice call and WhatsApp message at the two biggest stall points, with hand-off to an account manager. Run with a 20% holdout.A, CProduct (Merchant Growth) + Ops

How value is counted: merchants still transacting at day 30 × assumed monthly GMV for their segment (micro ₹60k, small ₹4.5 lakh, mid ₹25 lakh) × net revenue rate. Each month's signups add a layer of revenue that then decays with churn. Build cost: engineering weeks at ₹1.5 lakh each. Running cost: two voice calls (₹8 each) and three WhatsApp messages (₹0.80 each) per nudged merchant. All assumptions, all fictional.

How the program runs

  • Weekly, 30 minutes: metric first (2 min), then red items only, each ending with an owner and a date; decisions are written into the log in the meeting, not after.
  • Escalate to the CPO when: a decision is more than 3 working days late, an item is more than 7 days past due with no new date, or a guardrail is breached.
  • Every red needs a root cause. The weekly note below traces each red item back to the item actually causing it, so leadership sees two problems, not four.

How long the nudge test (L4) needs to run

Randomise by merchant at signup, not by week, because signup mix shifts week to week. 5% two-sided significance, 80% power. Unequal split handled by sizing the smaller (holdout) arm.

9. SQL I'd run first

Table and column names are assumptions about a typical onboarding schema, not PayU's.

-- 1. Funnel and 14-day first-payment rate by weekly signup cohort
WITH m AS (
  SELECT merchant_id, signup_at, segment, integration_path, source,
         docs_submitted_at, kyc_approved_at, live_at, kyc_status
  FROM merchant_onboarding
  WHERE signup_at >= DATE '2026-09-01' AND is_test = FALSE
),
fp AS (                                  -- first successful customer payment
  SELECT t.merchant_id, MIN(t.created_at) AS first_payment_at
  FROM transactions t
  JOIN m ON m.merchant_id = t.merchant_id
  WHERE t.status = 'SUCCESS'
    AND t.payer_vpa_or_card_hash NOT IN (SELECT hash FROM merchant_own_instruments WHERE merchant_id = t.merchant_id)
  GROUP BY t.merchant_id
)
SELECT DATE_TRUNC('week', m.signup_at)                         AS cohort_week,
       COUNT(*)                                                AS signups,
       COUNT(*) FILTER (WHERE kyc_status = 'REJECTED_POLICY')  AS policy_rejections,
       COUNT(docs_submitted_at)                                AS docs_submitted,
       COUNT(kyc_approved_at)                                  AS kyc_approved,
       COUNT(live_at)                                          AS live,
       COUNT(fp.first_payment_at)                              AS first_payment_ever,
       ROUND(100.0 * COUNT(*) FILTER (WHERE fp.first_payment_at <= m.signup_at + INTERVAL '14 days')
             / NULLIF(COUNT(*) FILTER (WHERE kyc_status IS DISTINCT FROM 'REJECTED_POLICY'), 0), 1) AS rate_14d_pct
FROM m LEFT JOIN fp USING (merchant_id)
GROUP BY 1 ORDER BY 1;

-- 2. Where merchants stall: last step reached and the open reason code
SELECT COALESCE(r.reason_code, s.last_step) AS stall_point, COUNT(*) AS merchants,
       ROUND(100.0 * COUNT(*) / SUM(COUNT(*)) OVER (), 1) AS pct
FROM merchant_last_step s
LEFT JOIN kyc_rework r ON r.merchant_id = s.merchant_id AND r.resolved_at IS NULL
WHERE s.first_payment_at IS NULL AND s.kyc_status IS DISTINCT FROM 'REJECTED_POLICY'
GROUP BY 1 ORDER BY merchants DESC;

-- 3. Rework completion by reason (does L1 work?)
SELECT reason_code,
       COUNT(*) AS sent_back,
       ROUND(100.0 * AVG(CASE WHEN resolved_at <= raised_at + INTERVAL '7 days' THEN 1 ELSE 0 END), 1) AS fixed_in_7d_pct,
       PERCENTILE_CONT(0.5) WITHIN GROUP (ORDER BY EXTRACT(EPOCH FROM resolved_at - raised_at) / 86400) AS median_days_to_fix
FROM kyc_rework GROUP BY 1 ORDER BY sent_back DESC;

-- 4. Guardrail: 60-day chargeback and fraud rate, simplified vs standard route
SELECT o.dd_route,
       COUNT(DISTINCT o.merchant_id) AS merchants,
       ROUND(10000.0 * COUNT(c.id) / NULLIF(COUNT(t.id), 0), 2) AS chargebacks_per_10k_txn,
       COUNT(DISTINCT f.merchant_id) AS merchants_flagged_fraud
FROM merchant_onboarding o
JOIN transactions t ON t.merchant_id = o.merchant_id AND t.created_at < o.live_at + INTERVAL '60 days'
LEFT JOIN chargebacks c ON c.transaction_id = t.id
LEFT JOIN fraud_flags f ON f.merchant_id = o.merchant_id AND f.flagged_at < o.live_at + INTERVAL '60 days'
WHERE o.live_at IS NOT NULL
GROUP BY 1;

10. Day one with real data: what I'd check before trusting any of this

  1. Definitions. Does "signup" mean account created, or lead qualified? Are partner sub-merchants counted? Agree this before any target is set.
  2. Self-payments. Many merchants pay themselves ₹1 to test. If those count as "first payment", the north star is inflated.
  3. Event gaps. KYC vendor callbacks and onboarding events often don't join cleanly. I'd reconcile dashboard counts to the settlement report before showing a number to leadership.
  4. Which stall is biggest. My simulation puts "never started documents" first. Real data could say otherwise, and the lever order would change.
  5. What already exists. PayU's voice assistant already calls leads. L4 should build on it and measure it with a holdout, not duplicate it.
  6. Compliance position. Whether the simplified route is already in use, and how, is Compliance's call. L2 stays off the plan until they decide.

11. Limits (read this before the numbers)

  • The data is generated by my own assumptions, so the simulation can only show what those assumptions imply. It shows how I'd structure, size and run the work, not what PayU's real numbers are.
  • Lever effects are my estimates, not measured results. The point of the plan is to measure them with holdouts.
  • The value model ignores sales cost recovered, support cost and seasonality, and assumes GMV per merchant by segment.
  • No AI model was run. The weekly note uses fixed rules.
  • Engine and tracker rules are checked by 23 automated tests (node tests/run.js).
