Stop the Farms
Product Manager, Fraud Risk & Abuse Prevention · Tata Neu · work sample by Aditya Mathur

Stop the coin farms. Not the NeuCard holders.

Tata Neu is betting on loyalty and financial services. That makes NeuCoins worth stealing, and makes your best customers expensive to get wrong. This page replays 60 days of a synthetic NeuCoin ledger through two risk policies, event by event, and scores each on the coins it saves and the good customers it hurts.

Independent work on synthetic data. Not affiliated with Tata Digital. The “blunt rules” are a hypothetical starting point, not a description of Tata Neu’s actual controls.

The short version
  1. Abuse here isn’t one problem. It’s five: referral farms, earn-and-return, account takeover, UPI coin cycling, and look-alikes that are actually genuine (phone upgraders, people moving city, shop owners who return damaged stock).
  2. Single-signal rules catch … of abused coins but restrict … genuine customers, … of them NeuCard holders. Once customer harm is priced in, they lose money.
  3. A layered policy gives each pattern its own response, using the least friction that stops the loss: withhold the bonus, hold the coins until the return window closes, cap UPI coins, verify the person, or send the order to a human. It saves … and restricts … genuine customers.
  4. Tenured NeuCard holders are never auto-restricted. Their risky orders wait for a human review with a 24-hour SLA (… cases), so a takeover still can’t spend the coins.
  5. What it still misses is on the page: rings that share nothing visible, and earn-and-return orders just under the hold threshold.
01 · Why this problem

Every NeuCoin is ₹1, and the people complaining loudest are your best customers.

The bet

Tata Digital is refocusing Tata Neu on loyalty and financial services (ETRetail, April 2026). More coins flowing through co-brand cards and UPI means more value to farm.

The money

Tata Digital reported a loss of about ₹4,974 Cr in FY26. NeuCoins are worth ₹1 each with no minimum order, so leaked coins are cash.

The other side

Customers publicly complain about restricted accounts and blocked NeuCoins, and Tata Digital’s help desk has a dedicated path: “Restricted Account (NeuCard Holder): escalate to TN_Risk.” False positives already cost support time and trust.

03 · The policy duel

Same 60 days, same people. Two policies, run in real time.

Each event (order, return, bonus, UPI payment, device change, coin burn) is processed in order, and the policy only sees what has happened so far. That’s how a live risk engine works, and it’s why a return-based rule fires after the coins are already gone.

06 · The response ladder

Use the least friction that stops the loss.

1AllowMost activity.
2Hold coinsBig orders, until the return window closes. Nothing is “wrong.”
3Don’t payWithhold a farmed bonus, cap repeated UPI coins. No block.
4VerifyNew device + new number + new address: confirm on the old number.
5Human reviewTenured NeuCard holders. That order waits, the account doesn’t.
6RestrictOnly proven rings. Always with an appeal path.
07 · If I joined

First 90 days, and the scorecard I’d report weekly.

  1. Weeks 1–3: size the losses, honestly. Coins lost by pattern, and the false positives: restricted accounts later reinstated on appeal, and support tickets tagged “NeuCoins blocked.” Both sides get a number before anything changes.
  2. Weeks 4–6: ship the cheapest fixes first. Holds on high-value orders until the return window closes, and capping repeated UPI coins. These save money without blocking anyone.
  3. Weeks 7–10: the NeuCard review lane with a 24-hour SLA, and step-up verification on the takeover pattern. Run it on 10% of traffic against a control.
  4. Weeks 11–13: graph signals for rings, starting with shared devices and addresses, then payment instruments. Write the restriction policy and appeal flow with Legal and Customer Support.

08 · What it still misses

Where this design is weak.

  • Rings that share nothing visible (fresh devices, scattered addresses). Next signals: payment instruments, KYC name similarity, and sign-up timing.
  • Earn-and-return just under the hold threshold. Lower the threshold and you delay more genuine buyers. The slider in section 04 shows the trade.
  • Takeovers that phish the OTP on the real number skip the strongest signal. They’re caught here only by the large-burn-after-new-device rule.
  • The simulation sets its own rates. It shows the mechanism and the trade-offs, not Tata Neu’s actual loss numbers.
