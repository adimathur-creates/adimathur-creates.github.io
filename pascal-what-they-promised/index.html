What did management actually promise?

A guidance ledger for earnings season, and the eval I’d want passing before it ships to an asset manager. Every guided number is traced to the line it came from, followed across six calls, and checked against what was reported.

The three companies, their calls and every number are fictional and written by me to plant the traps real transcripts contain. Nothing here is real company data or Pascal’s product.

The problem

Results season is a guidance problem, not a summary problem

An analyst at an AMC covering 25 companies has a few hours after each call to answer four questions: What is guidance now? Did it change? Is the change real or just new wording? What did management stop talking about?

A summary doesn’t answer those. A summary of a call where the CFO says “high single digits” reads fine. It doesn’t tell you that last quarter he said “double-digit”, which means guidance was cut without anyone saying the word.

Where an AI goes wrong here

It rarely misses guidance outright. It records the wrong thing with confidence: an analyst’s “should we assume 12%?”, ₹25 billion read as ₹25 crore, an adjusted margin compared with a reported one, last year’s actual logged as next year’s target.

Each of those goes straight into a model and an IC note. For a product that sells auditability, one wrong number costs more trust than ten missing ones.

Product decisions I made up front:
  • A wrong number is worse than a missing one, so precision and zero critical errors gate the launch before recall does.
  • No citation, no number. Every figure links to the line it came from, and the line must contain that figure.
  • Silence is a signal. Guidance that disappears gets flagged like a cut.
  • Beat or miss only on the same basis. Reported vs adjusted gets two verdicts, never one blended one.
  • Verbal ranges follow a house convention the customer can edit (“mid-teens” = 14–16% by default).

    7 · How I’d run it in production

    A model extracts. Rules filter and check. An analyst confirms the soft cases.

    Filter

    Extract

    A strong model reads only the flagged lines plus two lines of context either side, and returns records with a citation. It never sees the whole call at once, so it can’t blend two answers.

    Check

    Seven checks that need no answer key: speaker, citation contains the number, forward-looking language, unit and plausible range, period, basis, floor vs range. Any failure blocks the record.

    Confirm

    Cost and time (every input is an assumption; change it)

    Ship gates and what I’d watch

    • Launch gate: zero critical errors on the eval set and on one shadow season; recall ≥ 95%; every figure grounded.
    • Guardrail: wrong numbers found by analysts per 100 records. Target zero; one is an incident with a written root cause.
    • Value: minutes from transcript to brief; analyst edits per call; share of records auto-accepted.
    • Signal quality: share of flags (cuts, drops, basis shifts) the analyst opens; share they dismiss.
    • Learning loop: every correction becomes a test case; the eval set should grow every season.

    Rollout

    • Season 1, shadow: one AMC team’s coverage list. Analysts keep their own tracker; we compare after every call.
    • Go/no-go: zero wrong numbers in shadow, and the team says the brief saved time on at least most calls.
    • Season 2: live for that team with the review queue on. Add investor presentations and results releases as sources.
    • Then: push flags into the model refresh and IC-memo flows, so a quiet cut shows up where the analyst already works.

    What I’d ask the first five customers

    • Where does guidance live today: an Excel tracker, Bloomberg, call notes? Who updates it, and when?
    • Which hurts more: a missed change, or a false alarm you have to clear?
    • What does “mid-teens” mean on your desk? Is it the same for every analyst?
    • Do you want the brief at the end of the call, or after the results release numbers are in?
    • Who signs off a number before it goes into the model?
    Limits

    What this is not

    • The corpus is tiny (18 short calls, 91 lines) and written by me. Real calls run an hour, have messy speaker labels, and put guidance in slides and tables too.
    • The careful extractor is a rule set tuned to this corpus. Section 4 shows how badly it does on lines it hasn’t seen. The point is the test set, the checks and the ledger logic, not the rules.
    • The runtime checks were also written against this corpus. They catch every naive error here; I would expect them to catch fewer on real output, which is why the review queue exists.
    • Verbal-range conventions vary by house. The defaults here are mine.
    • Cost figures are placeholders, not any vendor’s price list.
