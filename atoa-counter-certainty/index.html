Counter Certainty

A card machine says "Approved" in two seconds. Pay by Bank at a shop counter often cannot. This page works out what the counter screen should say at every second, when Atoa can safely say "hand it over", and what that costs. It includes a working simulator, a webhook contract and a test plan.

Aditya Mathur · October 2026 · prepared for the Atoa product team

Read this first. I do not work at Atoa and have no internal data. The simulator runs 120,000 synthetic in-store payments across 10 anonymised banks over 20 trading days. Every rate in it, including bank behaviour, fraud, pending share and customer patience, is my assumption and is listed on this page. Banks are anonymised because real per-bank data is not public. Industry facts come from Pay.UK, Open Banking and Atoa's own help centre, linked at the end.
1 · THE PROBLEM

"Has it gone through?"

Atoa's core bet is in-person payments for small UK businesses, including salons, garages, clinics, bars and shops. At the counter, the merchant has to decide in under a minute whether to hand over the goods. Cards solved this with an approval code. Pay by Bank has no equivalent yet.

  • Banks do not push status. A payment initiation provider has to keep asking the customer's bank for the status. Pay.UK names this as a gap in its 2025 "Certainty of Fate" work.
  • Some payments are accepted but not finished. About 5% of single immediate payments got a "qualified accept" between January 2024 and September 2025, per Pay.UK. The money can take until the end of the next business day.
  • Banks hold some payments for review. Atoa's own help centre says a customer's bank "may hold larger payments for manual review".

When the status is unclear, one of three bad things happens: the queue waits, the customer pays again by card and gets charged twice, or the merchant accepts a banking-app screenshot and hands over goods that are never paid for.

At the counter, how should Atoa decide what the merchant sees at every second of a Pay by Bank payment, and when it can safely say "release the goods", so that more sales complete, nobody is charged twice, and merchants stop losing goods to payments that never arrive, at a cost Atoa can carry?

2 · THE COUNTER MOMENT

Where the seconds go

0–12 sCustomer scans the QR code, checks the business name and amount, and picks their bank
12–35 sBank app opens; customer approves with Face ID or passcode. Some fail here: app errors, bank outages, extra checks
35–45 sThe bank has a status. Atoa only learns it on its next poll
45 s+The risky part. If the status is still "in process", today's screen keeps spinning. The customer shows their bank app. The merchant has to guess

Proposed: three counter states, never a spinner

£42.50 · Bank APaid

The money has arrived. Hand it over.

£42.50 · Bank CSafe to release

The bank accepted it and it is still settling. Atoa guarantees it. Hand it over.

£320.00 · Bank INot confirmed yet

Do not accept a screenshot. Take another payment method. If this one lands later, it is refunded automatically.

Rules for "Safe to release": the bank has accepted the payment; the amount is at or below a cap (£150 in the model); this bank's in-process payments complete at least 99% of the time; and the payer has completed a Pay by Bank payment with Atoa before. The last rule is the important one, as section 5 shows.

4 · TEST THE CHANGES

Switch them on and off

Every run replays the same 120,000 customers, so differences come from the change alone.

Stress test. I reran the model with two other random seeds, half and double the fraud rate, 60% more in-process payments, and 20% or 60% of merchants accepting screenshots. In every run, the recommended set cut merchant losses by over 75% and left no double charges unresolved. The guarantee for returning payers cost 0–1.1% of Atoa's fee revenue. Guaranteeing anyone cost 10–28%.

5 · THE GUARANTEE

Who you guarantee matters more than how much

Returning payers onlyAnyone

For returning payers, the cost stays under 1% of fee revenue at any cap, because genuine in-process payments almost always complete. For anyone, it climbs past 13% at £150 and past 50% at £400. A fraudster with someone else's bank login is always a first-time payer, and their payments are the ones banks hold and then reject.

Rules for the contract: decisions only ever move forward (NOT_CONFIRMED can become PAID, but PAID never goes back); each event carries an idempotency key so a till that receives it twice acts once; and GET /payments/{id}/decision returns the same object for tills that poll instead of listening.

7 · RECOMMENDATION

What I would ship, in order

  1. Three-state counter screen and automatic refunds

    Ship the "Not confirmed yet" state and auto-refund first. They need no guarantee and no change in risk appetite, and they remove the two problems merchants and customers feel most: unpaid goods and double charges.

  2. "Safe to release" for returning payers, £150 cap

    In the model it costs about 0.6% of fee revenue and turns most in-process waits into instant decisions. It needs sign-off from risk, finance and compliance, because Atoa is taking on a loss it does not take today.

  3. Confirm from the incoming credit

    A small gain on its own, but it makes "Paid" honest: the money has actually arrived. It also feeds the bank completion rates the guarantee depends on.

  4. Hold the bank health warning

    In the model it sends more customers to cards than it saves. A better version may tell customers "your bank is slow, it may take a minute" rather than steer them away. That needs a test, not a launch.

8 · SPEC FOR THE FIRST RELEASE

Three-state counter and auto-refund

Users
  • Shop staff: need a yes or no they can act on in seconds.
  • Customer: needs to know they will not pay twice.
  • Merchant owner: needs a record of every decision for disputes.
User story

As a member of staff at the till, I want the screen to tell me clearly whether I can hand the goods over, so I never have to judge a customer's banking-app screenshot.

P0Counter states PAID, SAFE_TO_RELEASE (behind a flag), NOT_CONFIRMED and FAILED, in the Atoa app and through the webhook to POS partners.
P0After 20 seconds without a final status, show NOT_CONFIRMED with "do not accept a screenshot" and a one-tap switch to card or a new link.
P0Link any later payment from the same till and amount within 10 minutes; if both settle, refund the bank payment automatically and notify the customer by SMS.
P0Decision log per payment: every status seen, when it was seen, and what the screen showed.
P1Per-bank completion rates for in-process payments, refreshed daily, as an input to the guarantee rules.
P1Merchant setting to require PAID only, for businesses that prefer to wait.
Metrics
  • Main: share of in-store payments decided within 45 seconds.
  • Merchant losses: goods released on payments that never arrived, per 1,000 payments.
  • Double charges left unresolved after 24 hours.
  • Guardrails: Pay by Bank share of counter payments, support contacts per 1,000 payments, guarantee loss as % of fees (below 2%).
How to test it
  • Randomise by merchant, not by payment, so staff see one consistent screen.
  • Run for 4 weeks across a mix of verticals (salons, garages, bars, clinics).
  • Turn on the guarantee for half of the test merchants only after two clean weeks.
Risks
  • Fraudsters learn the rules. Mitigation: returning-payer rule, cap, per-merchant limits and a daily loss alarm.
  • Refunds need the payer's account details. Mitigation: capture them from the payment and confirm the route before launch.
Open questions for the team
  • Does a guarantee change Atoa's regulatory or capital position?
  • How often does the "in process" status appear per bank today?
  • If Pay.UK's rule changes land, how much of this problem goes away?
9 · FIRST 90 DAYS

How I would run this

Days 1–30

Pull status logs per bank. Rebuild this model on real data. Stand at three merchant counters at peak, and read every support ticket that says "paid" and "not received".

Days 31–60

Ship the decision log and the NOT_CONFIRMED state. Build auto-refund with engineering, and write the webhook docs for POS partners. Start the merchant-level test.

Days 61–90

Read out the test. Bring the guarantee proposal to risk and compliance with real completion rates. Scale what worked.

10 · LIMITS

What this model does not know

  • Every rate is an assumption: bank behaviour, fraud share, pending share, patience and screenshot habits. The ranking held in every stress test, but the sizes will change with real data.
  • Card fallback is assumed to be available. Merchants without a terminal lose the sale instead.
  • Refunds are treated as always possible and free. In Pay by Bank, refunds need the payer's account details, which is a design problem of its own.
