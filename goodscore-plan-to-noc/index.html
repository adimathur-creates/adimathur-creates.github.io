Plan to NOC

GoodScore now leads with "Improve: settles overdue accounts", in five steps: Detect, Reduce, Plan, Close, Rebuild. The member is paying for one outcome, a better score. The score only moves when a plan is paid in full and the lender reports it to the bureau. I modelled that whole path, measured it in SQL, and found where it breaks and which three fixes to test first.

1. The problem, defined

Problem statement. Of the overdue accounts GoodScore detects for a member, too few end as a closure the member can see on their credit report. The ones that end invisible cost support tickets and subscriptions, and the ones that end "SETTLED" move the score far less than the member was promised.

Why it is worth a product analyst's first quarter

  • Subscription retention. Members pay monthly to watch the score move. A closure they can't see looks like no progress.
  • The loan marketplace. GoodScore shows loan offers "as the score rises" and quotes 5–6 months to 750+. Every closure that doesn't land pushes a member's first loan out.
  • The lender network. 30 partner lenders take part in settlements. I assume they value closures that complete, so completion rate is also what keeps lenders offering waivers. This is an assumption, not a known term.

What public evidence says

  • Reviews praise converting overdues into EMIs, and one member describes 3 of 7 loans closed, with NOCs, after seven months. The path works, slowly.
  • Another member says a loan removed from CIBIL still showed as active with dues in the app, with promised callbacks never made. That is the "paid but invisible" gap below.
  • The homepage process ends at "Close (lender confirmation and NOC tracking) → Rebuild". There is no step for "the bureau shows it", which is where the score actually changes.

North-star and the tree under it

Visible closures within 45 days of final payment, per 100 detected overdue accounts (now: ) = offer rate lender returns a plan of 100 × acceptance member says yes → × completion every instalment paid → × NOC rate lender confirms closure → × visible-in-45 bureau shows it within 45 days → ( by 10 Nov) Guardrails: share of 30–89 DPD closures reported SETTLED · tickets per 100 paid-off cancellations within 60 days · lender counter-offer rejections

Why 45 days and not "eventually": lenders report to bureaus on a monthly cycle, so a closure that is right should appear within one or two cycles. Past 45 days the member has usually already called support.

7. A plan-risk score at the moment of acceptance

  

    

      
A logistic regression on what is known when the member taps "accept": EMI-to-income, where income came from, payday alignment, autopay mandate, DPD bucket, lender type, tenure, number of overdue accounts and city tier. No feature uses anything that happens later. Trained on  finished EMI plans, tested on  held out.

  • Flagging the top 20% reaches % of all breaks. That is the queue for the fit check in test 2: these plans get a renegotiation offer before acceptance, not a reminder after a miss.
    • 
        
  • It is deliberately a simple, explainable model. A credit-ops person has to be able to tell a member why their plan was flagged.

Test 1 · Payday-aligned first due date

      

        
Unit
Accepted plan, members with salary SMS (payday known)

        
Arms
Control: due in 7 days. Treatment: due 2 days after next payday, never more than 25 days out.

        
Primary
First instalment paid by its due date

        
Then
Completion at the last due date

        
Guardrails
Lender rejection of the later date; days from acceptance to closure

        
Size
 plans per arm to detect +4 pts (α 0.05, power 0.8)

        
Ship if
First-instalment lift ≥ 3 pts and lender rejection stays under 5%

      

    
Test 2 · Fit check before acceptance

      

        
Unit
Offer, within partner lenders that agree to counter-offers

        
Arms
Control: today's plan. Treatment: plans in the top-20% risk band, or over 35% of income, get a longer-tenure counter-offer, or a "close the smallest account first" sequence for members with 3+ overdues.

        
Primary
Plan completion

        
Guardrails
Acceptance rate (a longer plan may put people off); lender withdrawal of offers; total days to closure

        
Size
 plans per arm to detect +4 pts

        
Ship if
Completion lift ≥ 3 pts with acceptance not down more than 2 pts

      

    
Test 3 · Closure tracker with an expected date

      

        
Unit
Paid-off account

        
Arms
Control: today. Treatment: "Expected on your report by {date}" from that lender's measured lag, a push when the NOC arrives, and an automatic bureau dispute at day 45 if it still hasn't shown.

        
Primary
Accounts with any "paid, still showing active" ticket

        
Secondary
Cancellation within 60 days

        
Size
 per arm to detect a 30% drop. The effect is large, so two weeks of payoffs is enough.

        
Ship if
Tickets down ≥ 20% and the expected dates are right for at least 80% of accounts

      

    
Rule change · Close by default at 30–89 DPD

      

        
What
For 30–89 DPD, default to "pay in full in EMIs → CLOSED". Show the waiver option with a plain line: "This will show as SETTLED on your report and lifts your score less."

        
Measure
SETTLED share at 30–89 DPD; score change at 90 days; acceptance rate

        
Why not a test
It is a disclosure members should get anyway. Measure it before and after, and watch acceptance.

Where I could be wrong

      
    
        
  • If lenders, not GoodScore, set the first due date, test 1 becomes a lender negotiation, not a settings change.
    • 
        
  • If the real bureau lag is mostly under 30 days, Leak B shrinks and the tracker matters less than NOC chasing.
    • 
        
  • The SETTLED penalty differs by bureau and score model. The direction is safe; the size is not.
    • 
        
  • Members who choose autopay are probably more organised to begin with, so the mandate effect in raw data overstates what a default would do. That is why it sits behind tests 1 and 2.
