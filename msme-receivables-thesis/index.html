Who gets India's small suppliers paid?

Rs 8.1 lakh crore is owed to India's small businesses. Between 2023 and 2026 the government built new rails to fix it. This memo asks which kind of company wins the gap those rails leave, and whether any of them can return an early-stage fund.

The short version

The question. Should a seed fund back a company in MSME late payments in 2027, and what shape must that company have to matter to a $50 million fund?

  • Yes, but not on the supplier's side. The supplier has the pain and no budget. Since 43B(h), and more since the October 2026 amendment, the buyer carries the cost of paying late. Money and urgency now sit with the buyer.
  • The best shape is a buyer-led working-capital platform: sell compliance to the buyer's finance team first, then finance the same approved invoices early. It scores highest under my weights, and it stays top when I remove the fund-return criterion entirely.
  • No shape returns Fund II on its own in my base case. The platform gets closest ( of the fund at 10% exit ownership). To reach 1x alone it needs about Rs 68,229 cr of invoices financed a year, roughly a fifth of what TReDS discounted in FY26. That is the bar I would hold a founder to in the first meeting.
  • Claim-to-cash is the most underbuilt shape and the one the 2026 amendment helps most, but it needs a balance sheet. Worth a pre-seed (Origami-size) bet only with a lending partner already signed.

1Why now

For most of the last 20 years the law said "pay within 45 days" and nobody had a reason to obey. Three things changed in three years: the buyer now pays a tax cost for being late, filing became digital and mandatory, and an award now has teeth.

  1. Apr 2023Section 43B(h) applies to invoices from 1 April 2023: paying a micro or small supplier late now costs the buyer its tax deduction for that year. [source]
  2. Nov 2024TReDS made mandatory for every company above Rs 250 crore turnover (was Rs 500 crore) and all CPSEs, by 31 March 2025. [source]
  3. Jun 2025MSME Online Dispute Resolution portal goes live. From 15 Oct 2025 every new delayed-payment case must be filed on it. [source]
  4. Jan 2026Economic Survey puts delayed dues at about Rs 8.1 lakh crore and pushes cash-flow-based lending. [source]
  5. Feb 2026Budget: credit guarantee for TReDS invoice discounting, mandatory TReDS settlement for CPSE purchases, a secondary market for TReDS receivables. [source]
  6. Sep 2026Credit guarantee cover for TReDS financing goes live. [source]
  7. Oct 2026MSMED (Amendment) Act in force: 90-day mediation, 75% pre-deposit to appeal an award, awards enforceable as debt under IBC, disclosure penalties up to Rs 1 lakh. [source]
  8. Dec 2026Amended council (MSEFC) process takes effect on 15 December. [source]

2What the new rails still miss

RailWhat it fixesWhat it leaves out
TReDSFast, cheap early payment on invoices the buyer has accepted. Volume is up 8.7x since FY22. [source]Buyers below Rs 250 crore turnover, private buyers who are not onboarded, and any invoice the buyer has not accepted or disputes. [source]
Section 43B(h)Gives the buyer a tax reason to pay micro and small suppliers within 45 days. [source]Only covers suppliers the buyer can identify as micro or small. Some buyers respond by avoiding registered suppliers or squeezing terms. [source]
ODR portal + 2026 amendmentOne digital route, 90-day mediation, 75% pre-deposit to appeal, awards enforceable under IBC. [source]The supplier still has to build the claim: invoice dates, acceptance proof, the buyer's own words, the interest maths. And filing can damage the relationship. [source]
Samadhaan (legacy)Nine years of filings: 2,56,892 applications. [source]Only 58,148 cases disposed. All claims ever filed equal 6.8% of today's stuck dues: almost nobody files.
The gap, in one line: the rails work for accepted invoices from large buyers. The money that is stuck is mostly in disputed, unaccepted, or mid-sized-buyer invoices, held by suppliers who cannot assemble a claim and fear losing the customer.

3Six shapes a company could take

I did not start from a list of startups. I started from who pays and what the company owns, then checked who is already there.

1

Supplier collections copilot

Helps a small supplier chase, document and escalate its own unpaid invoices. This is what Recourse is.

Who pays: The supplier (micro or small business)

Already here: Easebuzz EasyCollect (8i portfolio)

2

Buyer-side MSME payables compliance

Tells a large buyer which vendors are micro or small, when each invoice must be paid, what it costs to be late, and routes the payment on time or through TReDS.

Who pays: The buyer's finance team (CFO, controller)

Already here: CashFlo

3

Claim-to-cash

Turns an unpaid invoice into a filing-ready claim, runs it through the ODR portal, and pays the supplier early against a strong claim.

Who pays: Success fee from recovered money, plus a finance margin

Already here: Presolv360

4

Receivables finance beyond TReDS

Early payment on invoices TReDS does not reach: buyers below Rs 250 crore, small-to-small trade, invoices not yet accepted.

Who pays: Financiers pay a share of the spread

Already here: RXIL, M1xchange, Invoicemart, C2treds, DTX (KredX), KredX, Vayana

5

B2B payment-behaviour bureau

A shared record of which buyers pay late, sold to suppliers before they extend credit and to lenders.

Who pays: Suppliers (reports) and lenders (data)

Already here: Recordent

6

Buyer-led working-capital platform

Starts as buyer compliance (shape 2), then finances the same invoices early (shape 4) because it already sees them, approved, inside the buyer.

Who pays: Buyer subscription plus financing spread

5Can any of them return the fund?

An early-stage fund needs a few companies that each return the whole fund. Fund II targets $50 million [source], and 8i has said it aims to own 10-15% of category winners at exit [source]. At 10% ownership, one company has to exit at to return the fund. For scale: the 12x M2P exit returned Rs 115.9 crore [source], roughly the size of Fund I.

6The call

Take the meeting

Buyer-led working-capital platform. A founder who has sold to CFOs, with a plan to finance invoices through bank or NBFC partners from year two. First proof I would ask for: 10 buyers paying for compliance, and one lender ready to fund their approved invoices.

Pre-seed only, with conditions

Claim-to-cash. The amendment makes a good claim worth money, and nobody owns the supplier's path from evidence to award. Back it only with a lending partner signed, and price the cheque for an Origami-size bet [source].

Pass

Supplier copilots (including my own Recourse), the payment bureau and plain invoice-discounting marketplaces. Low budget, slow cold start, or crowded with funded players. The copilot also overlaps with Easebuzz, already in 8i's portfolio.

7What I learned diligencing my own company

I built Recourse: a supplier pastes a WhatsApp thread into Claude, Recourse builds an evidence timeline, works out the legal due date and the MSMED interest, and refuses to let anyone escalate a weak claim. The product works. Run through the model above, the business does not clear the bar.

My own numbers

  • Rs 999 a month, Rs 0 AI cost (customers bring their own Claude), about 69% contribution at 100 customers.
  • Support is the real cost: Rs 250 per customer per month, break-even at about 56 minutes of support a month.
  • Year-one target: 150 businesses, about Rs 18 lakh ARR. These are planning targets, not results.

What I would change

  • Sell the same evidence engine to the buyer's finance team, where a late payment now has a tax and penalty cost.
  • Use the claim pack as the input to financing, not just to escalation: a verified, enforceable claim is an asset a lender can price.
  • Keep the rule that software never changes money or escalates on its own. That trust design is the part worth keeping.

8How I would find this founder first

The best founder for this company is probably not raising yet. Here is where I would look, and what I would ask.

Where to look

  1. Chartered accountants. Companies must disclose dues to micro and small suppliers. CAs see which clients are late and which suppliers are owed. Ask 20 CA partners who in their network is building on this.
  2. People leaving TReDS exchanges. Ops and sales staff at RXIL, M1xchange, Invoicemart, C2treds and DTX know exactly which invoices TReDS cannot take.
  3. ODR and council practitioners. Lawyers and conciliators on the new portal see which claims succeed.
  4. Tally and Zoho Books builders. Plug-ins that read payables and Udyam status are the first product a buyer-side founder ships.
  5. Origami applicants. Re-read every past fintech application for anyone who touched payables.

First-call questions

  1. Who signs the cheque, and what does a late payment cost them this year?
  2. How do you know a vendor is micro or small, invoice by invoice?
  3. What happens to a disputed invoice in your product?
  4. Which lender will finance your buyers' approved invoices, and on what terms?
  5. How many support minutes does a customer need each month?
  6. What do you do when a buyer drops a registered supplier to avoid 43B(h)?

Reasons I would walk away

  • Revenue depends on suppliers paying a subscription.
  • No plan for disputed or unaccepted invoices.
  • Financing plan is "we will raise debt later".

9How this helps the existing portfolio

TransBnk

Escrow-as-a-service and reconciliation with banks. A claim-to-cash or platform company needs escrow for pre-deposits, settlements and financed payouts.

Jurisphere

Legal AI for research, drafting and document review, with a lawyer network. Drafting claims and handling contested cases is its kind of work.

Easebuzz

Collects for SMEs with payment links and reminders. A supplier-side copilot competes with it; a buyer-side product could send it payments.

10Method and limits

  • Every fact links to a public source; several are news reports of official documents rather than the documents themselves. Industry figures (Recordent) are not official statistics.
  • The revenue model is deliberately simple: units times price. It is a way to argue about assumptions in the open, not a forecast.
  • Scores for seven criteria are my judgement. I tested that the top shape does not depend on the fund-return weight, and that the supplier copilot ranks last on every single criterion except capital (see the tests).
  • I did not count companies with formal MSME procurement; the 25,000 buyers figure is the weakest input. It is the first thing I would check with TReDS exchange data.
  • Exit multiples are assumptions and change with the market.
