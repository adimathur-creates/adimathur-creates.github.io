1 · Built for Volopay

Before 30 November: a GST input-credit check for Volopay India customers

Open the live tool →
Problem
Indian companies lose GST credit on spend that already runs through Volopay: our GSTIN never reaches the invoice, the vendor never reports it, or the claim misses the deadline. For FY 2025-26 that deadline is 30 November 2026 (CGST Act s.16(4)).
Decision
v1 is a CSV tool customer success runs for five India customers before 30 November. GSP integration, auto-chasing and point-of-spend GSTIN capture wait until v1 shows real money coming back.
Built
A spend export and the GSTR-2B go in. It matches invoices in three passes (invoice number, value and date, then invoice number across GSTINs to catch a vendor-master typo), sorts credit by next action, writes vendor chase emails and an IMS action for every supplier invoice. JavaScript, runs in the browser, 20 automated tests.
Result
On a fictional ₹2.57 Cr spend year: ₹7.5 L still recoverable with 28 days left, two vendors holding ₹4.7 L; 3,563 of 3,563 filed invoices matched correctly across 20 synthetic years.

2 · Internal tools and automations for finance and payments teams

Settlement Leak Finder

Reconciliation · built for Osfin
Problem
Orders, the gateway’s settlement report and the bank statement never agree on IDs, timing or amounts, so one-key matching buries real losses under false alarms.
Built
Reconciles the three files in one pass, shows which rule closed each match, audits every fee against the contract, and turns what is left into a claim pack for the gateway.
Result
UTR-only matching raises 9 alarms and 8 are false; 91% of the claimable money sat in one missing settlement, not in fees.

4 · How I use AI coding tools, and check them

I build with Claude Code, and I treat its output as a first draft that has to pass tests and my own review. Three bugs found in review, before shipping:

  • Supply Desk counted Plaid’s POST read endpoints as money-moving and read YouTube “subscriptions” as webhooks. I added a rule for read-only POSTs and tightened webhook detection, then re-ran all 32 specs.
  • Before 30 November first left a vendor-master GSTIN typo as “invalid GSTIN”, though the invoices were sitting in GSTR-2B under the right number. A third matching pass, on invoice number and value across GSTINs, now finds them; a test checks it.
  • Every build validates against something I can check independently: the GSTIN check character against the GST portal’s published sample; matches against known answers; totals that must reconcile to the rupee.
