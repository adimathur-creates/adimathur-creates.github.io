Absent isn’t terminated.

A provider group sends this month’s roster. It’s in its own layout, half the addresses are written differently from the directory, two rows contradict each other, one NPI has a typo, and this time the file is missing half the group. A naive diff turns that into dozens of wrongful terminations and hundreds of fake “changes.” This page takes 12 synthetic rosters through the part HiLabs says carries 75% of the value: everything after standardising.

Independent work on synthetic data. Not affiliated with HiLabs; no client or patient data. NPIs are synthetic but pass the real NPI check digit.

The short version
  1. Standardising a roster is the easy quarter. The hard part is deciding which rows should change the directory, which go back to the group, and what to do about providers who simply aren’t on the file.
  2. A naive diff (raw text compare, missing means terminated, no validation) on these 12 rosters: ….
  3. The pipeline maps each layout, normalises addresses and phones, checks NPIs against the check digit and NPPES, catches rows that contradict each other, and requires evidence before terminating anyone. Result: ….
  4. Two lines of defence against wrongful terminations: a completeness check (if a “full” roster is missing over 20% of a group, hold every termination and ask the group to resend), then claims (anyone who billed in the last 90 days goes to review, not termination).
  5. Every rejected row goes back to the group with a reason and a fix, and the directory-update clock (2 business days) is tracked per roster. The SQL behind each check runs live below.
01 · Why rosters

The data arrives wrong, in a different shape every time.

The clock

The 2021 Consolidated Appropriations Act (No Surprises Act) expects directory updates within 2 business days of receiving a change. Roster backlogs break that clock.

The 75%

HiLabs’ own write-up says standardisation is only about 25% of the value of roster automation. The rest is rules, balance-and-controls, loading the provider database and feedback to providers.

The trap

Groups send partial files, delta files labelled as full, and rows that disagree with each other. Treat “missing” as “terminated” and you remove real doctors: the reverse of a ghost network.

Every synthetic provider has a hidden truth: same, moved, new phone, panel opened or closed, left the group, NPI deactivated, licence lapsed, or new hire. Both approaches see only the files.

The pipeline’s 2 missed terminations are leavers in the half-sent roster: held on purpose until the group resends. That’s the trade: a short delay instead of removing 20+ active providers.

05 · How I’d deploy this at a new payer client

First 30 days, and what I’d report each week.

Days 1–5
Discovery with provider data ops. Which groups send rosters, how often, in which layouts, full or delta? Who loads them today, how big is the backlog, and how often is the 2-day clock missed? Get 3 months of past rosters and the directory as it looked each month.
Days 6–12
Replay history. Run past rosters through the pipeline and compare against what ops actually loaded. Count wrongful terminations and missed changes in the client’s own data. That number makes the case to their VP.
Days 13–20
Agree the rules with the client. The completeness threshold, what goes to review vs back to the group, and who signs off on terminations. Write them as configuration, not code, so the next client is a setting change.
Days 21–30
Run in parallel with their current process for the 5 highest-volume groups, then switch over group by group. Feedback files go to groups weekly so the data improves at the source.
Weekly metricWhy
Rosters loaded within 2 business daysThe regulatory clock. The headline.
Touchless share of rowsOps capacity freed up. It should rise as groups fix their files.
Terminations reversed within 30 daysThe wrongful-termination rate. Target zero.
Rows rejected per group, over timeWhether feedback files are working at the source.
Review queue ageReview is only safe if it’s fast.
06 · Limits

What this doesn’t do yet.

  • Header mapping is a synonym list. Real rosters need a model for unseen layouts, plus a human confirming the mapping the first time a group sends a new one.
  • Address matching is rule-based. Production needs USPS standardisation and geocoding: “Suite 4” vs “Floor 2, Suite 4” still slips through.
  • Specialty is compared as text. It should be mapped to taxonomy codes and checked against NPPES.
  • The simulation sets its own error rates. The replay against a client’s real history (day 6) is what gives real numbers.
