Time to Trade

Where a new institutional client waits between "application complete" and "ready to trade", and the four changes I would test first, without weakening a single control.

Aditya Mathur · October 2026 · prepared for the StoneX onboarding modernisation programme

Read this first. I do not work at StoneX and have no internal data. Every number in the model comes from a synthetic event log of 2,400 onboarding cases I generated, using assumptions written out on this page. The method is the point: on day one I would swap the synthetic log for real workflow timestamps and rerun it. Company facts come from public filings and press, linked at the end.
1 · THE PROBLEM

One question for the programme

Of the elapsed time between a complete client application and a client being ready to trade, how much is work and how much is waiting, and which changes remove the most waiting while every control stays in place?

The programme brief describes replacing fragmented and duplicated processes with one controlled flow, from due diligence and KYC/AML through account creation, entitlements, credit and risk setup, reference data and trading readiness. That is a long relay with many handoffs. In relays like this, the time usually goes into the gaps between teams, not into the work itself. If that is true here, the biggest gains come from changing the order of the relay, not from making each team faster.

Why now
  • StoneX closed the R.J. O'Brien deal on 31 July 2025, adding 75,000+ client accounts and about 300 introducing brokers, plus the Benchmark acquisition. More books means more duplicated onboarding paths to fold into one.
  • The firm already runs Fenergo for client lifecycle management, so workflow timestamps should exist to measure this properly.
  • Management said in the Q3 FY2026 call that AI has moved to an enterprise-wide rollout, so an AI-assisted step is in scope.
Scope
  • In: new institutional and commercial clients, from complete application to trading-ready.
  • Out: self-directed retail apps, periodic KYC refresh, payments clients. Same method applies later.
  • Main metric: median and 90th-percentile business days to trading-ready.
  • Guardrails: zero accounts enabled before KYC and credit approval; enhanced due diligence (EDD) rules unchanged; wasted setup hours on rejected clients tracked.

What the pattern says

  • Work is only about a tenth of elapsed time. The rest is queues and waiting on the client.
  • KYC carries the largest single block, but credit, entitlements and reference data together wait longer, and they cannot start until KYC ends.
  • Most KYC send-backs are document problems that could be caught at intake, before an analyst picks up the case.
4 · LEVERS

Test the fixes on the same cases

Each switch reruns all 2,400 cases with one change. The same random draws are reused, so differences come from the change alone. Your filters above still apply.

Median business days, each lever alone vs today

I also reran the model with every wait assumption moved 30% up or down, and rework durations moved from 0.6x to 1.5x. Parallel build was the biggest lever in every run, cutting between 5 and 8 days off the median. The other three each moved the median by under one day. Their value shows up in fewer client chasers and fewer analyst hours, not in headline days.

5 · RECOMMENDATION

What I would ship, in this order

  1. Build in parallel, gate at go-live (pilot)

    Biggest effect on time to trade. It changes workflow order, not policy, so control risk is low if the gate is hard-coded. The cost is setup work on clients later rejected, which the model shows as a few hours per rejected case. Pilot on one business line, one region, low and standard risk only.

  2. Intake completeness pre-check

    Small effect on days, large effect on client experience: fewer mid-review chasers and fewer cases bouncing back to KYC analysts. It also makes the pilot above cleaner, because parallel setup only starts on complete packs.

  3. Collect once, reuse everywhere

    The structural fix the programme already describes. Slow to build because it touches every downstream system, so it runs as a platform track behind the first two.

  4. Entitlement templates

    Cheap and quick for commercial and clearing clients. Needs sign-off from client services and exchange-permission owners on the standard bundles.

6 · SPEC FOR LEVER 1

Build in parallel, gate at go-live

User story

As an onboarding lead, I want account, credit, entitlements and SSI setup to start as soon as a low or standard risk client's pack is complete, so that the client can trade the day KYC is approved instead of a week later.

Users

Onboarding analysts, KYC analysts, credit officers, client services, static data team, relationship managers, and the client waiting on the other side.

P0A trading-enable gate that requires KYC approved, credit approved, entitlements done and SSIs verified. No manual override without a second approver and a logged reason.
P0Setup tasks are created in a pending state: accounts exist but are blocked for trading and for receiving client money until the gate passes.
P0Eligibility rule: low or standard provisional risk tier and a pack marked complete. High-risk and EDD clients stay sequential.
P0If KYC rejects, every pending setup task is cancelled and the accounts are closed automatically, with an audit entry.
P1One status view per client showing each stage, its owner and its age, for the RM and the client.
P1If the KYC risk tier is raised to high mid-review, pending setup pauses and the case moves to the EDD path.
Acceptance checks
  • Zero accounts enabled before both approvals, tested on every pilot case.
  • A rejected client leaves no open account or live entitlement within one business day.
  • A risk-tier upgrade pauses all pending setup within the same day.
Metrics
  • Main: median and P90 days to trading-ready for pilot clients vs a matched control group.
  • Guardrails: control breaches (target 0), wasted setup hours per rejected client, KYC decision quality on QA sample.
  • Read-out: after 8 weeks or 150 pilot clients, whichever comes first.
Risks
  • Compliance may see early setup as pre-judging KYC. Mitigation: pending accounts are inert, and the gate is system-enforced, not a checklist.
  • Credit teams may resist work on clients who may not pass KYC. Mitigation: track and share wasted hours weekly.
Dependencies
  • Workflow change in the client lifecycle platform to fork tasks after pack completion.
  • Back-office ability to hold an account in a blocked state.
  • Sign-off from compliance, credit risk and each entity's MLRO.
7 · THE AI STEP

A pre-check that flags, never approves

What it does
  • Reads the uploaded pack and checks it against the document list for that client type and region.
  • Flags missing items, expired documents, unsigned tax forms and ownership charts that do not reach 100%.
  • Drafts one clear request to the client listing everything missing, for an analyst to send.
What it never does
  • Approve, reject or risk-rate a client.
  • Clear a screening or adverse media hit.
  • Send anything to a client without a person reviewing it.
Evaluation before go-live
  • A labelled test set of past packs, split by client type and region, with known missing or expired documents.
  • Go-live bar: catches at least 60% of preventable document issues, false alarms on no more than 10% of complete packs, and zero cases where a flag is skipped without a person signing it off.
  • Every flag, the document it points to and the analyst's decision go into the audit record.
  • Weekly sample review by KYC QA for the first quarter. If the catch rate drops below the bar, the check falls back to a plain checklist.
8 · FIRST 90 DAYS

How I would run this as the APM

Days 1–30

Pull real stage timestamps for one workstream. Rebuild this model on that data. Sit with KYC, credit and static data teams for a day each and map the real flow, including the work-arounds.

Days 31–60

Share the real wait-vs-work picture with the senior PM and stakeholders. Agree the pilot scope for parallel build. Write the requirements and the test set for the pre-check.

Days 61–90

Run the pilot with a matched control group. Report days saved, chasers avoided, wasted hours and control breaches. Decide with the team whether to scale, change or stop.

9 · LIMITS AND OPEN QUESTIONS

What this model cannot tell you

  • All durations, rework rates and rejection rates are my assumptions. The ranking held under the stress test above, but the size of each saving will change with real data.
  • The model treats each stage as one queue. Real teams share people across stages and entities, which can make parallel work slower than shown.
  • I do not know which steps the firm already runs in parallel, or how each entity's rules differ. Those are the first questions I would ask.
Questions I would bring to the team
  • Which onboarding paths still run outside the client lifecycle platform after the RJO and Benchmark integrations?
  • Where do the onboarding timestamps live today, and are queue times recorded or only completion times?
  • Which setup steps do compliance teams in each entity already allow before final KYC approval?
  • How many times is the same client data keyed today, and into which systems?
