The 2027 pile-up

RBI’s new export rules took effect on 1 October 2026. Every open export bill in India now sits under one of several deadline rules, depending on when it shipped. The transition creates a quiet few months, then two sets of deadlines land together from March to August 2027. An exporter who misses one by more than a year can only ship against full advance or an LC, and banks must now monitor every bill continuously. I built the clock that sorts a book of bills into what to do now, and the plan that turns it into Bluno’s next bank partnerships.

    For exporters

    Proceeds unpaid more than a year past the due date mean every future export needs full advance or an irrevocable LC. Bills up to ₹10 lakh can now be closed on a declaration. Software no longer uses SOFTEX: services file an EDF within 30 days of the invoice month ending.

    For banks

    Authorised dealer banks must run continuous monitoring of every bill’s realisation and upload documents to EDPMS within 5 working days. They now decide extensions, reductions, set-offs and third-party payments themselves, under published policies, and may not charge penalties for delays.

    3 · The growth play

    Sell the 2027 wave to banks before it arrives

    1. Bank partnerships: “Your 2027 peak, handled”

      Banks must now monitor every bill continuously. Bluno offers the trade desk a portfolio view like the one above, plus the exporter-side work that clears it: extension requests with reasons, declaration batches for small bills, and eBRC follow-through. Lead with banks that have large SME export books in Bluno’s hubs, and the pitch has a date on it.

    2. Exporter acquisition: a free realisation check

      An exporter uploads their outstanding-bills report and gets this action list in a minute. It shows the cliff, the extensions to ask for and the bills to declare. The free check is the hook, and the subscription is what they need for the next nine months.

    3. Distribution through people exporters already trust

      Export promotion councils, CA firms that file for exporters, and freight forwarders who already hold the shipping data. Each can run the check for their clients, with Bluno as the system that does the work.

    4. Timing

      December to February is the window: banks are planning for the new rules, and almost nothing is due yet. By March the desks are firefighting and won’t take meetings.

    4 · How we would know

    Partnerships

    Banks signed, and the share of each partner’s export book monitored through Bluno.

    Exporter growth

    Free checks run, the share that convert to paid, and exporters onboarded per bank partner.

    Outcome

    Bills cleared before their due date, and exporters kept off the advance-or-LC restriction. That number is the case study the next bank wants to see.

    Speed

    Time to close a declaration batch and to get an extension approved, before and after Bluno.

    5 · What I don’t know

    • How much of this Bluno already does. It likely handles EDPMS reconciliation and eBRCs today; the new parts here are the transition clock, the cliff list and the bank-side portfolio view.
    • How each bank reads the transition for pre-October bills, and whether the one-year rule applies to them. Banks publish their own policies now, so this may differ by partner.
    • Bluno’s current bank partners and their commercial terms, which decide whether the bank or the exporter pays.
