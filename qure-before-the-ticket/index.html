Before the Ticket

How I would investigate a chest X-ray nodule model's failures before asking R&D for anything: which gaps are real, which come from the reference standard, which come from a new site, and what each one should turn into.

Aditya Mathur · October 2026 · written for Vatsal Khetan

Read this first. Everything here is synthetic and illustrative. There is no Qure.ai model, data or clinical result on this page, and I have no clinical experience. I simulated 12,600 chest X-rays across four sites, a three-reader reference standard and a CT-confirmed subset, with failure modes built in on purpose, so the method can be shown end to end.     9 · The validation study I would propose next

    Reference standard

    Three readers plus adjudication, with CT confirmation for every model-reader disagreement. Small and retrocardiac nodules are where the readers are weakest, so that is where confirmation pays most.

    Pre-specified subgroups

    Size, location, view, vendor, site, image quality, age and sex, written down before unblinding. Sample size per subgroup set by the interval we need, about 139 positives for ±5 points at 90%.

    Enrichment

    At 5% prevalence, 139 positives means about 2,800 images per subgroup. Enriching for the weak subgroups (retrocardiac, under 6 mm, portable AP) from archives costs far less.

    Why I built this

    You described the role as closer to a research PM. My earlier eval work was for an LLM, not a medical model: a 31-case evaluation framework for a support assistant, with hard safety gates and a judge that is trusted only after it agrees with human labels. Its scorecard was illustrative too, not a production result. What carries over is the habit this page shows: decide what would prove a failure is real before asking anyone to fix it. At Stripe the most useful thing I did was find a data pipeline that was failing silently, which is the same instinct applied to data.

    Limits
    • Synthetic data with failure modes put there on purpose. A real analysis starts from Qure.ai's data and its own reference standard.
    • The simulator knows the true label for every image, which a real team never does. I use it only to show why the CT subset and adjudication matter.
    • Drift is measured with a population stability index on model scores. It shows that something changed, not why; the why needs image review.
    • I am not a clinician. Clinical impact, care pathways and regulatory claims would come from the clinical and regulatory teams.
    Method

    AUROC by rank sums; Wilson 95% intervals; population stability index on 11 score bins against sites S1–S3, months 1–6; positives needed = 1.96² × 0.9 × 0.1 / 0.05². Thirteen automated checks confirm each finding across five independent synthetic runs.
