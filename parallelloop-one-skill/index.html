One Skill, Three Clients

The question that decides how Parallel Loop gets valued: does the AI Stack become a product, or does every client become a custom build? I took one workflow your own industry pages name, chasing missing and invalid documents, and built it once. It is rules-first, with a small AI step, a confidence gate and evals. Then I ran it unchanged for an insurer, a lender and a consumer durables maker.

Aditya Mathur · October 2026 · prepared for the Parallel Loop team

Read this first. I do not work at Parallel Loop and have no client data. The AI step is simulated: each document reading comes with a confidence score and a known error rate, and the golden dataset of 1,000 cases per client is synthetic. Everything else runs for real in your browser: the shared engine, the three rule packs, the evals and the time model. Every assumption is on this page.
1 · WHY THIS PROBLEM

The same workflow shows up on three of your industry pages

  • Financial services: "Onboarding delays revenue… new clients wait while staff chase missing items." Use case: "show missing documents and pending approvals so each team knows its next step."
  • Insurance: "Handlers must gather and verify documents before they can progress." Use case: "claims document tracking."
  • Retail and consumer: Havells is working with you on sales teams and channel partners. Every new dealer starts with a document pack and a credit check.

Your CTO has said about 90% of a workflow should be codified rules, with AI kept for the roughly 10% that needs classification or judgement. Investors are asking whether the stack becomes reusable or every engagement turns bespoke. This prototype tests both ideas on one real workflow.

Can one document-chase skill, built once with deterministic rules and a narrow AI step, serve very different clients by changing only a small rule pack, while never passing an incomplete file and getting files ready faster than people chasing by hand?

2 · HOW THE SKILL WORKS

Rules do the deciding. AI only reads.

AIRead each document. Classify it and read the fields the checks need. Every reading carries a confidence score.
GATEConfidence gate. Below the threshold, a person checks that one item. The AI never decides alone when it is unsure.
RULEApply the client's pack. Which documents this case needs, from whom, and the checks each must pass (names match, not expired, covers 6 months).
RULEHigh-value sign-off. Above a set value, a named person signs before the file moves on.
OUTOne status, one owner, one next action. Chase every owner at once, with daily reminders. Every step goes into the audit trail.

Reuse: the shared engine is 133 lines. Each client pack is 21 to 23 lines of rules. Adding a client means writing its pack and its golden test set. The engine, the confidence gate, the audit trail and the evals stay the same.

4 · EVALS

Test it before it touches a real file

Each client gets a golden set of 1,000 cases with known answers. The engine runs on every case, and the evals count what matters to the client.

ThresholdWrongly passed / 1,000Files to a personWrong chasesMedian days

Same engine, three different answers. With sign-off on, the insurer reaches zero wrongly passed files at a threshold of 0.80, sending 23% of files to a person. The lender only gets there at 0.95, sending 77%. The dealer workflow never reaches zero at any threshold, which means its checks need a stronger rule (for example, matching the GST and PAN names against each other), not a stricter model. A default threshold would have been wrong for all three. That is why every client needs its own golden set.

Stress test. I reran the evals with another seed, and with the AI's error rate at 2% and 8% instead of 4%. In every run and every client, the median days to a ready file fell from 6–8 to 3. Wrongly passed files stayed between 0 and 3 per 1,000. At 8% AI error, the share of files going to a person rose to about 40%, which is the signal to fix the model before go-live, not after.

6 · WHAT I WOULD DO AS PM

Make skills the unit of the product

  1. Track reuse as a product metric

    For every skill: lines of shared engine against lines of client pack, and days from kickoff to the first live workflow at each new client. If the pack share keeps rising, the stack is drifting into services.

  2. No skill goes live without a golden set

    Build 300 to 1,000 labelled cases from the client's first two weeks of real files. Set the confidence threshold per client from the evals, not by default. Every production miss becomes a new test case.

  3. Rules as data, written with the client

    Packs should be edited in the Skills Platform by the forward-deployed team and the client's process owner together, versioned like code, with the client signing off each version.

  4. Measure what the client feels

    Days to a ready file, files bounced back by the assessor, and wrong chases sent to customers. Not model accuracy on its own.

7 · FIRST TWO WEEKS AT A NEW CLIENT

How I would deploy this skill

Days 1–3

Sit with the people who chase files today. List every document, who sends it, and why files get bounced. Draft the pack.

Days 4–8

Label 300 recent files to build the golden set. Run the evals and agree a threshold and sign-off limit with the process owner.

Days 9–14

Run the skill alongside the team on live files without sending chases. Compare its decisions with theirs daily, then switch on chasing for one team.

8 · LIMITS

What this prototype does not prove

  • The AI step is simulated with a fixed error rate. A real model's errors cluster, for example on blurred photos or regional-language documents, and the golden set must reflect that.
  • Response rates and the "today" process are assumptions. The 6–8 day baseline needs checking against a client's real logs.
  • In production, rules live as data in a rules engine. Here they are small functions, to keep the prototype readable.
