Every renewal, on the Sav card

Subscriptions are the spend a card keeps month after month, and MyMoney already sees them in people’s linked bank accounts. But on a prepaid card with an AED 500 monthly gate and an AED 50 cap, “move your subscriptions for 5% cashback” is the wrong pitch. And moving them without changing how people top up makes renewals fail. I built the three pieces that fix this: a detector, a move plan priced on the card’s published rules, and a renewal guard. I’d ship them as one feature.

The short version, for the sample person

    1 · The problem

    A good idea that the card’s own rules make easy to get wrong

    Sav earns from its card through interchange and from Sav Pro subscriptions. A card that holds someone’s recurring merchants gets used every month and topped up on payday. A card that doesn’t is one people load once and forget. Three things make this harder on the Sav card than it looks:

    1 · The gate and the cap decide the value

    Cashback starts only after AED 500 of eligible spend in a month, and stops at AED 50 on Standard. Subscriptions alone rarely clear the gate. For someone who already spends a lot on the card, the cap is used up before the subscriptions arrive. The honest value of a move is different for every person, so the product has to calculate it, not advertise it.

    2 · Prepaid balances make renewals fail

    A credit card always has room for the AED 56 Netflix charge. A prepaid card has whatever was topped up. Move AED 500 of renewals onto a card that gets AED 400 every payday and some will decline. A failed renewal is silent: the person finds out when the show stops, and blames the card.

    3 · Bank data is messy

    The same merchant shows up as NETFLIX.COM 8665797172 NL one month and NETFLIX.COM AMSTERDAM the next. Fuel at AED 100 every week looks like a subscription; a Careem ride is not Careem Plus. Rent cheques, DEWA bills and your own top-ups repeat too. A subscription list with one wrong row stops being trusted.

    4 · 180 days of history hides annual plans

    MyMoney shows up to 180 days of transactions. An annual plan shows up once, or not at all, so it can’t be predicted from the data. The product should list one-off charges from known subscription merchants and ask the person, not guess.

    2 · The working tool

    Linked-account transactions in, an honest move plan and a renewal plan out

    It loads with a fictional Dubai resident: 180 days of transactions across an Emirates NBD debit card, a Mashreq credit card and the Sav card. Change any input and every number updates. You can also load your own CSV export; it runs in the browser and nothing is uploaded.

    CSV template

    Accuracy, honestly. Across 20 synthetic people the detector found all 272 planted subscriptions, called nothing else a subscription, and got every cycle right. It ignored same-amount fuel and coffee, a monthly supermarket shop, utility bills, rent cheques and top-up transfers. I wrote that noise myself, so real descriptors will be messier. The merchant list has 20 entries; unknown merchants are found by timing and amount alone. Current card FX fee defaults to 0, which is the conservative setting: it never credits a move with savings it can’t see.

    3 · What I would ship

    One feature in MyMoney: “Your subscriptions”

    1. Find them

      Run the detector on the accounts people have already linked. Show every subscription with its next date, and flag price rises, trials that turned paid and paying for two of the same thing. This part is useful even to someone who never moves a single one, and it’s the reason to open the app.

    2. Price each move honestly

      For each subscription: “earns AED 2.80 a month”, “optional: your cap is already used” or “keep it where it is: the FX fee costs more”. Show the gate effect separately, because for light users it’s most of the value.

    3. Make the move one tap

      Open each merchant’s billing page with the card details ready to paste. For anything billed through the App Store or Google Play, one change of payment method moves every app at once, so do those first.

    4. Guard the renewals

      On payday, suggest a top-up of the usual amount plus the next month’s renewals, and hold that part aside so coffee can’t spend it. If the held amount is short, nudge the day before a renewal, not after it fails.

    4 · How we would know

    Measure card use and failed renewals, not just clicks

    Main measure

    Share of people shown the feature who move at least one subscription within 30 days. Behind it: months with at least one card transaction, per person, at day 90.

    Guardrail

    Decline rate on recurring charges on the Sav card, and support contacts about failed renewals. If either rises against the holdout, the guard isn’t working and the rollout stops.

    Pro, done honestly

    Offer Pro only to people the tool says it pays for. Track their conversion and whether they’re still on Pro 60 days later. A Pro upgrade that doesn’t pay churns, and teaches people to distrust the next nudge.

    Test

    Half of eligible users see it, half don’t, for six weeks. Stop if fewer than 1 in 10 people shown it move anything in 30 days, or if recurring declines go up. Those thresholds are my starting guesses; Sav’s own data should set them.

    5 · What I don’t know

    The questions that would change the plan

    • The money on Sav’s side. I don’t know Sav’s interchange rate on this card. Once someone hits the cap, every extra dirham earns interchange with no cashback cost, so moving subscriptions may matter more to Sav than to the person. Only real numbers can say.
    • Held balances. Whether the card issuer allows part of a prepaid balance to be set aside for renewals. If it doesn’t, version 1 relies on nudges alone.
    • Moving cards at the merchant. Updating the card inside each merchant’s app is where most people will drop off. That is where I’d spend design time first.
    • Excluded categories. The listing says rent, wallet loads and transfers earn no cashback. I left utilities and telecom out of the move plan because I couldn’t confirm them; the Key Facts Statement decides.

    What I’d ask first: how many card users have at least one recurring merchant on the card today; whether recurring declines come before people stop using the card; and how many Standard users never clear the AED 500 gate or always hit the AED 50 cap.

    6 · How it’s built

    Linked-account CSV→clean descriptors · group by merchant→find the cycle · group prices · flag changes→move plan on the card rules→35-day balance simulation

    31 automated tests cover descriptor cleaning, detection accuracy against a known answer key across 20 synthetic people, every decoy type, price-rise, trial and overlap flags, month-end renewal dates, the gate, the caps, the FX logic, the Pro check, all three renewal strategies, and messy bank exports with title rows, dd/mm/yyyy dates and Dr/Cr columns.

    Honesty notes. The person, the accounts, every transaction and every price are invented. Real merchant and bank names appear only as realistic statement descriptors. Card rules come from kredit.ae’s Sav prepaid card page and tbreak’s July 2026 report on Sav’s Visa partnership, read 10 Nov 2026. I have no access to Sav’s data, customers or roadmap.
