Link out, without the leak: when web checkout pays for a US subscription app, and how to ship it

Since 2025, US iOS apps can send people to a web checkout and keep most of the 30% Apple used to take. The evidence on whether this works is split down the middle. This is how I'd decide it for a subscription app with numbers, test it without betting the app, and build the parts that quietly break: unlocking after payment, failed renewals, refunds and disputes.

The prize

Up to 30% of US iOS subscription revenue moves from Apple to the app, and cash arrives in days instead of about two months. For a bootstrapped studio that funds ads from revenue, both matter.

The catch

RevenueCat's own test saw 6% fewer paying customers and 36% more refunds on web. Others report the opposite. Leaving the app adds friction, and the app now owns billing problems Apple used to absorb.

The plan

Decide with a break-even number, not a feeling. Ship it as a 3-arm test, fix the four leaks first, and make sure it still pays if Apple wins a 15% fee.

2. The problem statement

For a US-heavy iOS subscription app, the App Store fee is one of the biggest costs after ad spend, and the web link is the first real chance to cut it. But the evidence is split, the legal fee could change, and moving billing to the web hands the app four new ways to lose money. Decide, with numbers, whether the link pays for this app, and if it does, ship it so that net revenue per paywall view rises without raising refunds, disputes or "I paid but nothing unlocked" tickets.

3. The decision model

The question isn't "is web cheaper?" It obviously is. It's: how much conversion can web lose before the fee saving is gone? That break-even number is what the team should argue about, and it depends on four things:

  • Plan mix. The 30¢ per charge costs 3.8% on a $7.99 weekly charge but 0.6% on a $49.99 annual one. Annual buyers are the best first candidates for web.
  • Who picks web. If only 20% of buyers choose the link, a bad web flow can't hurt much, and can't help much either.
  • Renewals. If web subscribers really renew better (Runna, Dipsea), it's a bigger win than the fee. If they renew better because they forgot (RevenueCat), it comes back as refunds and disputes.
  • Apple's future fee. The decision should still work at 15%, the top of what Apple has proposed.

What the middle-of-the-road numbers say (the calculator's defaults): with web converting 10% worse, the link adds about 6% net revenue per paywall view today. If Apple wins a 15% fee, the same setup loses 0.7%, and the break-even shrinks from "web can be 24% worse" to "8% worse". So the fee saving alone is fragile. What decides this is how good the handoff is (leak 1 below), and that's where I'd spend the design and engineering effort.

5. The four places web checkout leaks, and the fix for each

1

The handoff: leaving the app

Every extra screen between "Continue" and "Paid" costs buyers. The fix: open checkout with the email and plan already filled in, put Apple Pay first on Safari, and keep it to one page. Test whether a small web discount (say 15%) is worth sharing part of the saving to raise the web share.

2

The return: "I paid but nothing unlocked"

Payment succeeds on the web, but the app only finds out when a webhook arrives. If it's late or fails, a paying customer sees a paywall again, and that becomes a refund, a dispute or a one-star review. The fix is a clear sequence with a timeout at every step:

  • checkout_started → payment_succeeded → webhook_received → entitlement_granted → app_unlocked
  • The success page links straight back into the app. The app shows "Activating your plan…" and checks the entitlement for up to 60 seconds instead of showing the paywall.
  • A reconciliation job every 5 minutes finds payments with no entitlement and grants it. "Paid, not unlocked for over 5 minutes" is a metric that should be zero.
3

Failed renewals: churn Apple used to handle

On the App Store, Apple retries cards and runs a grace period. On web, that's now our job, and billing failures already drive 14% of App Store cancellations. The fix: smart retries, automatic card updates where the network supports them, an email before a card expires, a 7-day grace period with access kept, and an in-app banner with a one-tap "update card". Track recovery rate of failed renewals.

4

Refunds and disputes: revenue built on forgetting

RevenueCat saw more web subscribers set to renew and more refunds. That's people forgetting, and it returns as disputes, which cost a fee and can get a merchant penalised by card networks. The fix: a reminder email before every annual renewal, cancellation that is genuinely easy (California's auto-renewal law requires it online anyway), and a cancellation page that offers a pause or a cheaper plan once, not five times. I'd rather lose a renewal than earn a dispute.

6. The experiment

  • Who: new US iOS users who reach the paywall. Randomise by user; nothing here is shared between users, so a normal A/B test works.
  • Arms: A) App Store only. B) App Store + web link at the same price. C) App Store + web link 15% cheaper.
  • Primary metric: net revenue per paywall view, after all fees, refunds and disputes, read at day 35 (trials resolved, first weekly renewals in) and confirmed at day 95.
  • Guardrails: trial starts, refund rate, dispute rate, "paid not unlocked" count, failed-renewal recovery rate, App Store rating and support tickets per 1,000 buyers.
  • Size: to detect a 10% relative change in an 8% conversion rate (80% power, 5% significance), each arm needs about 18,500 paywall views. For an app with a million users, that's days, not weeks. The slow part is waiting for renewals, not traffic.
  • Decision rule, set before launch: ship B or C if net revenue per view beats A at day 35 and still beats it with Apple's fee set to 15% in the model, with no guardrail worse by more than its threshold.

7. SQL

PostgreSQL. Assumed tables: paywall_views, experiment_assignments, purchases (channel = 'APP_STORE' or 'WEB'), charges, refunds, disputes, entitlements.

1. Net revenue per paywall view by arm, day 35 
WITH v AS (
  SELECT ea.arm, pv.user_id, min(pv.viewed_at) AS first_view
  FROM paywall_views pv
  JOIN experiment_assignments ea ON ea.user_id = pv.user_id AND ea.experiment = 'web_link_us_ios'
  GROUP BY 1, 2
),
money AS (
  SELECT v.arm, v.user_id,
         coalesce(sum(c.net_amount_usd), 0)                         AS net_charges,   -- after store/processor fees
         coalesce(sum(r.amount_usd), 0)                             AS refunded,
         coalesce(sum(d.amount_usd + d.fee_usd), 0)                 AS dispute_cost
  FROM v
  LEFT JOIN charges  c ON c.user_id = v.user_id AND c.charged_at < v.first_view + interval '35 days'
  LEFT JOIN refunds  r ON r.charge_id = c.charge_id
  LEFT JOIN disputes d ON d.charge_id = c.charge_id
  GROUP BY 1, 2
)
SELECT arm,
       count(*)                                                      AS paywall_viewers,
       round(sum(net_charges - refunded - dispute_cost) / count(*), 4) AS net_rev_per_view,
       round(100.0 * avg((net_charges > 0)::int), 2)                 AS pct_paying
FROM money GROUP BY 1 ORDER BY 1;
2. Paid but not unlocked (should be zero) 
SELECT p.purchase_id, p.user_id, p.paid_at,
       e.granted_at,
       extract(epoch FROM (coalesce(e.granted_at, now()) - p.paid_at)) / 60 AS minutes_waiting
FROM purchases p
LEFT JOIN entitlements e ON e.purchase_id = p.purchase_id
WHERE p.channel = 'WEB'
  AND p.status = 'SUCCEEDED'
  AND (e.granted_at IS NULL OR e.granted_at > p.paid_at + interval '5 minutes')
ORDER BY p.paid_at DESC;
3. Failed renewals on web: how many we win back, and how fast 
SELECT date_trunc('week', f.failed_at)                                         AS week,
       count(*)                                                                AS failed_renewals,
       round(100.0 * avg((s.charged_at <= f.failed_at + interval '7 days')::int), 1)  AS pct_recovered_7d,
       round(100.0 * avg((s.charged_at <= f.failed_at + interval '14 days')::int), 1) AS pct_recovered_14d
FROM charges f
LEFT JOIN LATERAL (
  SELECT min(c2.charged_at) AS charged_at FROM charges c2
  WHERE c2.subscription_id = f.subscription_id AND c2.status = 'SUCCEEDED' AND c2.charged_at > f.failed_at
) s ON true
WHERE f.channel = 'WEB' AND f.status = 'FAILED' AND f.is_renewal
GROUP BY 1 ORDER BY 1;
4. Refund and dispute rates by channel and plan 
SELECT c.channel, c.plan,
       count(DISTINCT c.charge_id)                                                    AS charges,
       round(100.0 * count(DISTINCT r.charge_id) / count(DISTINCT c.charge_id), 2)    AS refund_rate_pct,
       round(100.0 * count(DISTINCT d.charge_id) / count(DISTINCT c.charge_id), 3)    AS dispute_rate_pct
FROM charges c
LEFT JOIN refunds  r ON r.charge_id = c.charge_id
LEFT JOIN disputes d ON d.charge_id = c.charge_id
WHERE c.status = 'SUCCEEDED' AND c.charged_at >= now() - interval '90 days'
GROUP BY 1, 2 ORDER BY 1, 2;
5. Did they forget? Refunds or disputes within 7 days of an annual renewal 
SELECT c.channel,
       (c.reminder_sent_at IS NOT NULL)                                              AS got_reminder,
       count(*)                                                                       AS annual_renewals,
       round(100.0 * avg((r.charge_id IS NOT NULL OR d.charge_id IS NOT NULL)::int), 2) AS pct_refunded_or_disputed_7d
FROM charges c
LEFT JOIN refunds  r ON r.charge_id = c.charge_id AND r.created_at <= c.charged_at + interval '7 days'
LEFT JOIN disputes d ON d.charge_id = c.charge_id AND d.created_at <= c.charged_at + interval '7 days'
WHERE c.plan = 'ANNUAL' AND c.is_renewal AND c.status = 'SUCCEEDED'
GROUP BY 1, 2;
-- If reminders don't lower this number, the cancellation flow is too hard, not the reminder too loud.

9. Risks and what would prove this wrong

  • The law moves. Apple's fee on link-outs is 0% today and may not stay there. Every decision is tested at 15%, and the build is cheap enough to switch off.
  • App review. One non-compliant flow can get an app pulled, as one was in April 2026. The App Store option stays visible, the disclosure is clear, and we never re-prompt after a "no".
  • Tax becomes our problem. On the App Store, Apple is the seller and handles US sales tax. On the web, we are, unless a merchant of record does it for a fee. That fee is in the calculator.
  • Fraud and trial abuse. Web trials invite stolen cards and repeat trials. Card checks, one trial per card and device, and dispute rate as a hard guardrail.
  • Attribution. Purchases that leave the app are harder to tie back to the ad that drove them. Pass the install's campaign ID into checkout so paid social still sees what works.
  • The honest null result. If web subscribers renew better only because they forget, the day-95 refund and dispute numbers will show it. Then the answer is "App Store only" for this app, and that's a fine answer.

At EnKash I owned the payments side of an acquiring product: checkout, payment links, transaction-status webhooks, refunds, settlement and reconciliation.
