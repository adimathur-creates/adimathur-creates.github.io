1 · THE CUSTOMER PROBLEM

Every failed UPI debit has a clock on it

UnifyApps' payments-bank story describes finance teams running UPI reconciliation and disputes on files and spreadsheets, against NPCI deadlines and nightly cutoffs, at millions of transactions a day. It went from discovery to production in 7 weeks. The rules that clock is set by:

CaseDeadline (T = transaction date)If missedSource
UPI to a person: debited, not creditedReversed by T+1₹100 a day to the customerRBI, 20 Sep 2019
UPI to a merchant: debited, merchant not creditedAuto-reversal by T+5₹100 a day to the customerRBI, 20 Sep 2019
Pending ("deemed") transactionsChargeback decided only on the beneficiary bank's credit confirmation (TCC) or return (RET), in the next settlement cycleRejected chargebacks, penaltiesNPCI, from 15 Feb 2025

How does a payments bank return every rupee it owes on failed UPI transactions inside RBI's deadlines, without growing the dispute team, and without ever auto-closing a fraud complaint?

2 · THE FINDING THAT CHANGES THE SCOPE

A faster dispute desk is not enough

The clock starts on the day of the transaction, not the day of the complaint. In the model, a quarter of customers complain two or more days later, so for person-to-person payments the deadline has already passed when the ticket arrives.

SetupOn time

So the scope is not "automate the dispute desk". It is "reconcile every day, and return owed money before anyone complains", plus a dispute desk for what remains. That is the reconciliation half of the payments-bank story, and it is where the money is.

4 · THE DESK, SIMULATED

Test the build before the customer does

Stress test. I reran the model with another seed, with 30% more and 30% fewer complaints, and with the AI wrong 4% or 12% of the time. On-time returns stayed at 99.6% in every run, and compensation fell from ₹1.9–50 lakh a month to ₹5,500–12,600. Fraud complaints auto-closed stayed at 0–1 in 36,000. With 30% more complaints, today's desk falls to 25% on time and about ₹50 lakh a month in compensation. A festive-season spike breaks the manual desk, not the built one.

6 · DISCOVERY WORKSHOP

90 minutes with finance, operations and compliance

Questions I would ask
  1. Walk me through one failed UPI debit, from switch to ledger. Who touches it?
  2. Where do the switch, core banking and NPCI files land, and when?
  3. How many complaints a day, and how many arrive after the deadline?
  4. Which reason codes do you use, and how often is the first one wrong?
  5. What did you pay in TAT compensation last quarter?
  6. How do you raise chargebacks today, and since February 2025 how often are they rejected?
  7. How is a fraud complaint spotted today, and by whom?
  8. What would compliance need to see before a rule acts without a person?
What I would ask them to send
  • One day of switch, core banking and NPCI files
  • 200 recent complaints with the final outcome, to use as the test set
  • The current reason-code list and SOPs
  • Last quarter's compensation and breach report
Workshop output

A one-page process map, the rule table on this page in their words, the first use case agreed, and the success measures signed by finance and compliance.

7 · SEVEN WEEKS TO PRODUCTION

The same timeline as the payments-bank story

Week 1Discovery workshop. Access to file drops. Process map and rule table signed off.
Week 2Data pipelines live in a test environment. One day of files reconciled and checked by hand against the bank's own result.
Week 3Sweep and decision rules built. Run on 30 historical days; every difference with the bank's actual outcome explained.
Week 4Complaint reader and reply drafter built. Tested on the 200-case set; threshold agreed with compliance. Fraud tripwire tested on every past fraud case.
Week 5Console and TAT board. Analysts work real cases in the console with automation in suggest-only mode.
Week 6Shadow run: the build decides, people act. Daily review of every disagreement.
Week 7Go-live for person-to-person disputes first, merchant disputes a week later. Hypercare: daily on-time rate, compensation, overrides and fraud referrals.
Go-live checklist
  • 30 historical days reconciled with zero unexplained differences
  • Zero fraud cases auto-closed on the test set
  • Rollback switch to suggest-only mode, tested
  • Audit log reviewed by compliance
Success measures
  • Owed money returned within RBI's deadline: above 99%
  • Monthly TAT compensation: down by more than 90%
  • Analyst hours on disputes: down by more than half
  • Fraud complaints auto-closed: zero
8 · THE 5-MINUTE DEMO

For the CFO and head of operations

  1. Start with their number. "Last quarter you paid this much in TAT compensation. Here is the breakdown by reason."
  2. Show the clock. One complaint that arrived after its deadline. "No dispute desk could have saved this one."
  3. Run the sweep live on yesterday's files. Owed transactions found and reversed before any complaint.
  4. Show a fraud complaint stopped by the tripwire, never touched by the AI.
  5. Open the audit log. "Every decision, the rule that made it, and who can override it."
9 · LIMITS

What this kit does not prove

  • Volumes, complaint timing, handling times and AI error rates are assumptions. The real numbers come from the workshop.
  • The sweep assumes files arrive daily and match on reference numbers. Many banks have gaps, and that is week 2's real work.
  • Fraud screening here is a simple tripwire. A bank's own fraud signals should feed it.
