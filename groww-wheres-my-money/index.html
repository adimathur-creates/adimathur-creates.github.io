Where's My Money

My bet: most "where is my money" questions arrive before the money is late. The first SQL below tests it. Answer those in seconds, from the customer's own account, with the exact time it will land. The ones that really are late skip the bot and go straight to a person, with the case already worked out.

Exchange complaints are the tip: they're what's left after support didn't settle it. The rules that answer most money questions are already public, but they're spread across help pages and blogs. Withdrawals have four time bands. Sale proceeds unlock at 10 AM the next trading day. IPO blocks clear when a UPI mandate expires. Equity fund redemptions pay T+2. A customer asking "kab aayega?" needs those rules applied to their own request time, and to holidays like Dussehra and Diwali. A general FAQ answer doesn't do that.

The one decision that matters: is it late yet?

Not due yet

  • The customer is anxious, but nothing is wrong.
  • Answer in chat with their due time and the rule behind it, plus a tracker.
  • Goal: no human contact, and no repeat contact before the due time.

Overdue or failed

  • Something is wrong. A bot reply here is what turns a ticket into an exchange complaint.
  • Skip the bot. Put it in the priority queue with the case packet: amounts, timestamps, the rule, how late it is.
  • Goal: the customer never has to explain twice.
message→intent (Hinglish-aware)→account state→published timeline + holiday calendar→verdict→answer or human

Two hard rules sit in front of everything. Any sign of unauthorised activity goes to the security team at once. Requests for advice ("which stock should I buy") are declined.

PRD: money-status resolution

Problem
Customers contact support to ask when their money will arrive. Most are not late yet, and they get a generic FAQ or wait for an agent. The ones that are late get the same treatment and escalate outside Groww.
Users
Customers waiting on a withdrawal, sale proceeds, IPO unblock, redemption or SIP. Support executives, who spend the first minutes of each late case rebuilding the timeline. CX leads, who answer for complaint volumes and turnaround times.
Goals
1) Close not-yet-due contacts in chat with a personal due time. 2) Get every overdue case to a person with full context. 3) Cut repeat contacts on the same transaction.
Non-goals
Fixing the payout rails themselves. Disputes about trade execution. Any investment guidance.

Metrics

  • North star: money-status contacts per 1,000 transacting users. The goal is fewer questions, not more bot conversations.
  • Not-due contacts closed in chat, with no repeat contact before the due time. A closed chat followed by a repeat contact doesn't count as resolved.
  • Overdue at first contact, by money type. This is a product health signal for the payments and ops teams, not a support metric.
  • Time to first human touch on overdue cases.
  • Guardrails: exchange and SCORES complaints per lakh users; CSAT on automated answers; zero hard-rule misses.

Experiment and rollout

  • Start with withdrawals and sale proceeds, the simplest rules with the most volume.
  • A 10% user holdout keeps today's flow for 4 weeks. Read contacts per 1,000, 7-day repeat contacts, CSAT, and complaints per lakh.
  • Add IPO next, timed before a large issue when IPO questions spike. Then mutual fund redemptions and SIP failures.

High-level design

  • Facts come only from the engine. The LLM never computes a date. If the account state API times out, the router sends the case to a person. It doesn't guess.
  • Spikes: market open, expiry days and big IPOs bring bursts. State reads are cached per user for 60 seconds, and the engine is a pure function, so it scales horizontally.
  • Idempotent hand-off: one open ticket per transaction. A second contact attaches to it instead of opening a new one.

SQL I'd run in week one

-- Share of money-status contacts that arrive before the money is due
SELECT c.intent,
       COUNT(*)                                             AS contacts,
       AVG(CASE WHEN c.created_at < m.due_at THEN 1.0 ELSE 0 END) AS share_not_due,
       AVG(CASE WHEN c.created_at > m.due_at THEN 1.0 ELSE 0 END) AS share_overdue
FROM support_contacts c
JOIN money_movements m ON m.id = c.transaction_id
WHERE c.created_at >= CURRENT_DATE - INTERVAL '30 days'
GROUP BY c.intent
ORDER BY contacts DESC;

-- Repeat contacts on the same transaction within 7 days (the real resolution test)
SELECT c.intent,
       AVG(CASE WHEN EXISTS (
             SELECT 1 FROM support_contacts r
             WHERE r.transaction_id = c.transaction_id
               AND r.created_at > c.created_at
               AND r.created_at <= c.created_at + INTERVAL '7 days') THEN 1.0 ELSE 0 END) AS repeat_7d
FROM support_contacts c
GROUP BY c.intent;

Risks

  • A wrong due time is worse than none. Rules change (the NSE circular moved sale proceeds to T+1, 10 AM). Every rule carries an owner and an effective date, and the eval set runs in CI.
  • Bank holidays differ from trading holidays, and by state. The calendar needs both. This prototype uses trading holidays for both and says so.
  • Deflection can hide incidents. During an outage like the August 2026 cloud incident, overdue cases spike together. The router should detect a spike and switch to an incident banner plus a queue, not hundreds of individual answers.
